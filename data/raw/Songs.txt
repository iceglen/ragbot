<div class="mw-content-ltr mw-parser-output" lang="en" dir="ltr"><p>Various <b>songs </b>appear and are mentioned throughout <a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games_trilogy" title="The Hunger Games trilogy"><i>The Hunger Games</i> trilogy</a>. Music, in general, holds importance for many characters: <a href="/wiki/Katniss_Everdeen" title="Katniss Everdeen">Katniss Everdeen</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Mr._Everdeen" class="mw-redirect" title="Mr. Everdeen">father</a> was renowned for his singing voice and taught many songs to his daughter; <a href="/wiki/Rue" title="Rue">Rue</a> described music as her favorite thing in the world, and music often plays a part in celebrations or important events throughout <a href="/wiki/Panem" title="Panem">Panem</a>.
</p><p>Music is prominent in the prequel novel <i><a href="/wiki/The_Ballad_of_Songbirds_and_Snakes" title="The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes">The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</a></i>, in which the character <a href="/wiki/Lucy_Gray_Baird" title="Lucy Gray Baird">Lucy Gray Baird</a> is a member of a performing group known as the <a href="/wiki/Covey" title="Covey">Covey</a>. She performs a number of songs throughout the novel. Several songs also play a role in <i><a href="/wiki/Sunrise_on_the_Reaping" title="Sunrise on the Reaping">Sunrise on the Reaping</a></i>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Featured_songs_in_the_trilogy">Featured songs in the trilogy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id=".22Deep_in_the_Meadow.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Deep_in_the_Meadow&quot;">"Deep in the Meadow"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>"<b>Deep in <a href="/wiki/The_Meadow" title="The Meadow">the Meadow</a></b>" (also known as "<b>Rue's Lullaby</b>") is a song sung by Katniss to Rue as the latter was nearing death (<a href="/wiki/Marvel" title="Marvel">Marvel</a> had speared her in the stomach)<sup id="cite_ref-THG_18_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG_18-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>. Because of her love of music<sup id="cite_ref-THG_16_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG_16-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>, Rue's last request was to hear Katniss sing. At first, Katniss did not know what to sing, but then remembered a lullaby she would sing to her sister <a href="/wiki/Primrose_Everdeen" title="Primrose Everdeen">Prim</a> when she was ill. The words are easy, soothing, and calming.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_18_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG_18-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>Katniss also found herself musing on the words of this song many years later as she watched her <a href="/wiki/Katniss_and_Peeta_Mellark%27s_daughter" title="Katniss and Peeta Mellark&#39;s daughter">own</a> <a href="/wiki/Katniss_and_Peeta_Mellark%27s_son" title="Katniss and Peeta Mellark&#39;s son">children</a> play in the Meadow in <a href="/wiki/District_12" title="District 12">District 12</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-MJ_E_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MJ_E-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>In <i>The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</i>, Lucy Gray Baird sings this song to <a href="/wiki/Maude_Ivory" class="mw-redirect" title="Maude Ivory">Maude Ivory</a>, having sung it to her in the past when she had croup. <a href="/wiki/Coriolanus_Snow" title="Coriolanus Snow">Coriolanus Snow</a> also listens to the song and finds it soothing, until <a href="/wiki/Mockingjay_(bird)" title="Mockingjay (bird)">mockingjays</a> pick up the melody, as he finds them disturbing.<sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_27_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_27-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span id=".22The_Hanging_Tree.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;The_Hanging_Tree&quot;">"The Hanging Tree"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div style="border:4px double #ffe600; border-radius:1em;"><center><i>Main article: <a href="/wiki/The_Hanging_Tree_(song)" title="The Hanging Tree (song)">The Hanging Tree (song)</a></i></center></div>
<p>"<b>The Hanging Tree</b>" was a song Katniss learned from her father when she was young, but <a href="/wiki/Mrs._Everdeen" class="mw-redirect" title="Mrs. Everdeen">Mrs. Everdeen</a> forbid them from singing it because of the song's morbid and rebellious lyrics. Katniss would not sing the song again for another ten years, when she sang it for <a href="/wiki/Pollux" title="Pollux">Pollux</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-MJ_9_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MJ_9-5"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>In <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Mockingjay_-_Part_1" title="The Hunger Games: Mockingjay - Part 1">The Hunger Games: Mockingjay - Part 1</a></i>, the song was surreptitiously recorded for a <a href="/wiki/Propo" title="Propo">propo</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Plutarch_Heavensbee" title="Plutarch Heavensbee">Plutarch Heavensbee</a> had the words changed from "necklace of rope" to "necklace of hope". After the propo aired, rebels took it up as a protest song as they blew up the hydroelectric dam in <a href="/wiki/District_5" title="District 5">District 5</a>, causing a massive power outage in <a href="/wiki/The_Capitol" title="The Capitol">the Capitol</a> that allowed rebel forces to carry out a <a href="/wiki/Rescue_mission" class="mw-redirect" title="Rescue mission">rescue mission</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-MJ_P1_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MJ_P1-6"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Origin">Origin</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>"The Hanging Tree" has a brief backstory in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Ballad_of_Songbirds_and_Snakes" title="The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes">The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</a></i>. It was one of many songs composed by <a href="/wiki/Lucy_Gray_Baird" title="Lucy Gray Baird">Lucy Gray Baird</a>, and it was written about the <a href="/wiki/Execution" title="Execution">execution</a> of <a href="/wiki/Arlo_Chance" title="Arlo Chance">Arlo Chance</a> at the real <a href="/wiki/Hanging_tree" title="Hanging tree">hanging tree</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_22_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_22-7"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_24_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_24-8"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>8<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The song was banned by <a href="/wiki/Commander_Hoff" title="Commander Hoff">Commander Hoff</a>, who considered it rebellious.<sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_30_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_30-9"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>9<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Soon after, Hoff was replaced by a new commander who banned all musical performances at <a href="/wiki/The_Hob" title="The Hob">the Hob</a> because music caused trouble.<sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_Epilogue_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_Epilogue-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Mentioned_songs">Mentioned songs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span id=".22Anthem.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Anthem&quot;">"<a href="/wiki/Anthem" class="mw-redirect" title="Anthem">Anthem</a>"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The national song of Panem at the time of the <a href="/wiki/74th_Hunger_Games" title="74th Hunger Games">74th Hunger Games</a>. It often accompanies Capitol propaganda media and is played at the end of each day in the Hunger Games <a href="/wiki/Arena" title="Arena">arena</a>.
</p>
<h4><span id=".22Rue.27s_four-note_song.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Rue's_four-note_song&quot;">"<a href="/wiki/Rue%27s_four-note_song" title="Rue&#39;s four-note song">Rue's four-note song</a>"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>This simple melody was developed by Rue as a signal to the <a href="/wiki/District_11" title="District 11">District 11</a> agricultural workers that the working day had ended. She whistled the tune to a group of mockingjays, who then carried it across the fields and orchards.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_16_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG_16-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Rue and Katniss later used this tune to signal each in the arena.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_17_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG_17-11"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span id=".22Mouse_song.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Mouse_song&quot;">"Mouse song"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/75th_Hunger_Games" title="75th Hunger Games">75th Hunger Games</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wiress" title="Wiress">Wiress</a> sang a children's song about a mouse and a clock.<sup id="cite_ref-CF_23_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF_23-12"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>12<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Catching_Fire" title="The Hunger Games: Catching Fire"><i>Catching Fire</i> film</a> interpreted this as the classic folk song "<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hickory_Dickory_Dock" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:Hickory Dickory Dock">Hickory Dickory Dock</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-CF_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF-13"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>13<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span id=".22District_4_wedding_song.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;District_4_wedding_song&quot;">"District 4 wedding song"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Music features prominently in the <a href="/wiki/Finnick_and_Annie%27s_wedding" title="Finnick and Annie&#39;s wedding">wedding</a> of <a href="/wiki/Finnick_Odair" title="Finnick Odair">Finnick Odair</a> and <a href="/wiki/Annie_Cresta" title="Annie Cresta">Annie Cresta</a>; the traditional wedding song of <a href="/wiki/District_4" title="District 4">District 4</a> compares marriage to a long sea voyage, and at the celebration following the ceremony, the refugees from District 12 show off their traditional music and dancing.<sup id="cite_ref-MJ_16_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MJ_16-14"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>14<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Featured_songs_in_The_Ballad_of_Songbirds_and_Snakes">Featured songs in <i>The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id=".22Gem_of_Panem.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Gem_of_Panem&quot;">"Gem of Panem"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div style="border:4px double #ffe600; border-radius:1em;"><center><i>Main article: <a href="/wiki/Gem_of_Panem" title="Gem of Panem">Gem of Panem</a></i></center></div>
<p>"<b>Gem of Panem</b>" is the anthem of Panem during the <a href="/wiki/Dark_Days" title="Dark Days">Dark Days</a>, and it remained so during the time of the <a href="/wiki/10th_Hunger_Games" title="10th Hunger Games">10th Hunger Games</a> as well. It is a favorite song of the <a href="/wiki/Grandma%27am" title="Grandma&#39;am">Grandma'am</a>, the grandmother of Coriolanus Snow, and he is talented at singing it as well due to having heard her sing it many times.
</p>

<h3><span id=".22Nothing_You_Can_Take_From_Me.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Nothing_You_Can_Take_From_Me&quot;">"Nothing You Can Take From Me"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>This song was performed by Lucy Gray Baird at her <a href="/wiki/The_Reaping" class="mw-redirect" title="The Reaping">reaping</a>. It was the first song that Coriolanus Snow ever heard her perform. As it was broadcast nationally throughout Panem and to <a href="/wiki/The_Capitol" title="The Capitol">the Capitol</a>, it made her an instant sensation, catching the attention of those who would be sponsors even before the <a href="/wiki/Sponsorship" title="Sponsorship">sponsorship</a> program was conceived of. The first lines of the song were actually sung by members of the Covey within the audience in District 12; Lucy Gray Baird then picked up the song and continued it.<sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_2_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_2-15"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>15<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span id=".22The_Ballad_of_Lucy_Gray_Baird.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;The_Ballad_of_Lucy_Gray_Baird&quot;">"The Ballad of Lucy Gray Baird"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>This song is performed by Lucy Gray Baird during her <a href="/wiki/Interviews" title="Interviews">interview</a> before the Games. Coriolanus Snow considered it a dark, moving and far too personal account of Lucy Gray's life, and found himself jealous.<sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_11_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_11-16"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>16<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Lucy Gray later explained to Snow that the last line specifically refers to <a href="/wiki/Billy_Taupe_Clade" title="Billy Taupe Clade">Billy Taupe Clade</a>, in that he bet that he could have the affections of both her and <a href="/wiki/Mayor_Lipp" title="Mayor Lipp">Mayor Lipp</a>'s daughter, Mayfair.<sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_27_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_27-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Forty years later, Snow arranged for <a href="/wiki/Haymitch_Abernathy" title="Haymitch Abernathy">Haymitch Abernathy</a> to see the footage of Lucy Gray singing this song.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>17<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In author <a href="/wiki/Suzanne_Collins" title="Suzanne Collins">Suzanne Collins</a>'s acknowledgements for <i>The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</i>, she states that is an entirely original song and is meant to be sung to a variation of a traditional ballad tune that has long accompanied tales of the unfortunate ends of rakes, bards, soldiers, cowboys, and the like. The title of the song is provided in these acknowledgements.<sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_Acknowledgements_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_Acknowledgements-18"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>18<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span id=".22Lucy_Gray.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Lucy_Gray&quot;">"Lucy Gray"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>This was the song from which Lucy Gray Baird got her name. <a href="/wiki/Maude_Ivory" class="mw-redirect" title="Maude Ivory">Maude Ivory</a> performed it for Snow during a day trip to the lake in the wilderness, a couple of hours from District 12. The song was about a girl who was sent out into a snowstorm and apparently ended up becoming a ghost girl. Snow disliked the song, as he both did not understand it entirely and thought it ridiculous.<sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_26_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_26-21"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>21<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The second to last verse was put on her grave marker in the <a href="/wiki/Covey" title="Covey">Covey</a>'s secret graveyard. <a href="/wiki/Haymitch_Abernathy" title="Haymitch Abernathy">Haymitch Abernathy</a> stumbled across it forty years after Lucy Gray vanished while looking for the grave of <a href="/wiki/Lenore_Dove_Baird" title="Lenore Dove Baird">Lenore Dove Baird</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>22<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>"<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Gray" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:Lucy Gray">Lucy Gray</a>" is actually originally a poem written by <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Wordsworth" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:William Wordsworth">William Wordsworth</a> in 1799. The poem, due to its age, is in the public domain. The lyrics featured in <i>The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</i> are slightly tweaked by author Suzanne Collins to fit the Covey.<sup id="cite_ref-Ballad_Acknowledgements_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ballad_Acknowledgements-18"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>18<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>

<br />

<i>She wandered up and down;</i><br />
<i>And many a hill did Lucy climb:</i><br />
<i>But never reached the town.</i><br />
<br />

<br />

<i>That spanned a deep ravine.</i><br />
<br />

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Raven">The Raven</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>"<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Raven" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:The Raven">The Raven</a>" by Edgar Allan Poe echoes throughout <i>Sunrise on the Reaping</i>, as it is Lenore Dove's name ballad.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Lyrics_17">Lyrics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<div class="poem">
<p><i>Once upon a midnight dreary, while I pondered, weak and weary,</i><br />
<i>Over many a quaint and curious volume of forgotten lore —</i><br />
<i>While I nodded, nearly napping, suddenly there came a tapping,</i><br />
<i>As of some one gently rapping, rapping at my chamber door —</i><br />
<i>“’Tis some visitor,” I muttered, “tapping at my chamber door —</i><br />
<i>Only this and nothing more.”</i><br />
<br />
<i>Ah, distinctly I remember it was in the bleak December;</i><br />
<i>And each separate dying ember wrought its ghost upon the floor.</i><br />
<i>Eagerly I wished the morrow; — vainly I had sought to borrow</i><br />
<i>From my books surcease of sorrow — sorrow for the lost Lenore —</i><br />
<i>For the rare and radiant maiden whom the angels name Lenore —</i><br />
<i>Nameless here for evermore.</i><br />
<br />
<i>And the silken, sad, uncertain rustling of each purple curtain</i><br />
<i>Thrilled me — filled me with fantastic terrors never felt before;</i><br />
<i>So that now, to still the beating of my heart, I stood repeating</i><br />
<i>“’Tis some visitor entreating entrance at my chamber door —</i><br />
<i>Some late visitor entreating entrance at my chamber door; —</i><br />
<i>This it is and nothing more.”</i><br />
<br />
<i>Presently my soul grew stronger; hesitating then no longer,</i><br />
<i>“Sir,” said I, “or Madam, truly your forgiveness I implore;</i><br />
<i>But the fact is I was napping, and so gently you came rapping,</i><br />
<i>And so faintly you came tapping, tapping at my chamber door,</i><br />
<i>That I scarce was sure I heard you” — here I opened wide the door; —</i><br />
<i>Darkness there and nothing more.</i><br />
<br />
<i>Deep into that darkness peering, long I stood there wondering, fearing,</i><br />
<i>Doubting, dreaming dreams no mortal ever dared to dream before;</i><br />
<i>But the silence was unbroken, and the stillness gave no token,</i><br />
<i>And the only word there spoken was the whispered word, “Lenore?”</i><br />
<i>This I whispered, and an echo murmured back the word, “Lenore!”</i><br />
<i>Merely this and nothing more.</i><br />
<br />
<i>Back into the chamber turning, all my soul within me burning,</i><br />
<i>Soon again I heard a tapping somewhat louder than before.</i><br />
<i>“Surely,” said I, “surely that is something at my window lattice;</i><br />
<i>Let me see, then, what thereat is, and this mystery explore —</i><br />
<i>Let my heart be still a moment and this mystery explore; —</i><br />
<i>’Tis the wind and nothing more!”</i><br />
<br />
<i>Open here I flung the shutter, when, with many a flirt and flutter,</i><br />
<i>In there stepped a stately Raven of the saintly days of yore;</i><br />
<i>Not the least obeisance made he; not a minute stopped or stayed he;</i><br />
<i>But, with mien of lord or lady, perched above my chamber door —</i><br />
<i>Perched upon a bust of Pallas just above my chamber door —</i><br />
<i>Perched, and sat, and nothing more.</i><br />
<br />
<i>Then this ebony bird beguiling my sad fancy into smiling,</i><br />
<i>By the grave and stern decorum of the countenance it wore,</i><br />
<i>“Though thy crest be shorn and shaven, thou,” I said, “art sure no craven,</i><br />
<i>Ghastly grim and ancient Raven wandering from the Nightly shore —</i><br />
<i>Tell me what thy lordly name is on the Night’s Plutonian shore!”</i><br />
<i>Quoth the Raven “Nevermore.”</i><br />
<br />
<i>Much I marvelled this ungainly fowl to hear discourse so plainly.</i><br />
<i>Though its answer little meaning — little relevancy bore;</i><br />
<i>For we cannot help agreeing that no living human being</i><br />
<i>Ever yet was blessed with seeing bird above his chamber door —</i><br />
<i>Bird or beast upon the sculptured bust above his chamber door,</i><br />
<i>With such name as “Nevermore.”</i><br />
<br />
<i>But the Raven, sitting lonely on the placid bust, spoke only</i><br />
<i>That one word, as if his soul in that one word he did outpour.</i><br />
<i>Nothing farther then he uttered — not a feather then he fluttered —</i><br />
<i>Till I scarcely more than muttered “Other friends have flown before —</i><br />
<i>On the morrow he will leave me, as my Hopes have flown before.”</i><br />
<i>Then the bird said “Nevermore.”</i><br />
<br />
<i>Startled at the stillness broken by reply so aptly spoken,</i><br />
<i>“Doubtless,” said I, “what it utters is its only stock and store</i><br />
<i>Caught from some unhappy master whom unmerciful Disaster</i><br />
<i>Followed fast and followed faster till his songs one burden bore —</i><br />
<i>Till the dirges of his Hope that melancholy burden bore —</i><br />
<i>Of ‘Never — nevermore.’”</i><br />
<br />
<i>But the Raven still beguiling my sad fancy into smiling,</i><br />
<i>Straight I wheeled a cushioned seat in front of bird, and bust and door;</i><br />
<i>Then, upon the velvet sinking, I betook myself to linking</i><br />
<i>Fancy unto fancy, thinking what this ominous bird of yore —</i><br />
<i>What this grim, ungainly, ghastly, gaunt, and ominous bird of yore</i><br />
<i>Meant in croaking “Nevermore.”</i><br />
<br />
<i>This I sat engaged in guessing, but no syllable expressing</i><br />
<i>To the fowl whose fiery eyes now burned into my bosom’s core;</i><br />
<i>This and more I sat divining, with my head at ease reclining</i><br />
<i>On the cushion’s velvet lining that the lamp-light gloated o’er,</i><br />
<i>But whose velvet-violet lining with the lamp-light gloating o’er,</i><br />
<i>She shall press, ah, nevermore!</i><br />
<br />
<i>Then, methought, the air grew denser, perfumed from an unseen censer</i><br />
<i>Swung by seraphim whose foot-falls tinkled on the tufted floor.</i><br />
<i>“Wretch,” I cried, “thy God hath lent thee — by these angels he hath sent thee</i><br />
<i>Respite — respite and nepenthe from thy memories of Lenore;</i><br />
<i>Quaff, oh quaff this kind nepenthe and forget this lost Lenore!”</i><br />
<i>Quoth the Raven “Nevermore.”</i><br />
<br />
<i>This I sat engaged in guessing, but no syllable expressing</i><br />
<i>To the fowl whose fiery eyes now burned into my bosom’s core;</i><br />
<i>This and more I sat divining, with my head at ease reclining</i><br />
<i>On the cushion’s velvet lining that the lamp-light gloated o’er,</i><br />
<i>But whose velvet-violet lining with the lamp-light gloating o’er,</i><br />
<i>She shall press, ah, nevermore!</i><br />
<br />
<br />
<i>“Prophet!” said I, “thing of evil! — prophet still, if bird or devil! —</i><br />
<i>Whether Tempter sent, or whether tempest tossed thee here ashore,</i><br />
<i>Desolate yet all undaunted, on this desert land enchanted —'<br /></i>
<i>On this home by Horror haunted — tell me truly, I implore —</i><br />
<i>Is there — is there balm in Gilead? — tell me — tell me, I implore!”</i><br />
<i>Quoth the Raven “Nevermore.”</i><br />
<br />
<br />
<i>“Prophet!” said I, “thing of evil! — prophet still, if bird or devil!</i><br />
<i>By that Heaven that bends above us — by that God we both adore —</i><br />
<i>Tell this soul with sorrow laden if, within the distant Aidenn,</i><br />
<i>It shall clasp a sainted maiden whom the angels name Lenore —</i><br />
<i>Clasp a rare and radiant maiden whom the angels name Lenore.”</i><br />
<i>Quoth the Raven “Nevermore.”</i><br />
<br />
<br />
<i>“Be that word our sign of parting, bird or fiend!” I shrieked, upstarting —</i><br />
<i>“Get thee back into the tempest and the Night’s Plutonian shore!</i><br />
<i>Leave no black plume as a token of that lie thy soul hath spoken!</i><br />
<i>Leave my loneliness unbroken! — quit the bust above my door!</i><br />
<i>Take thy beak from out my heart, and take thy form from off my door!”</i><br />
<i>Quoth the Raven “Nevermore.”</i><br />
<br />
<i>And the Raven, never flitting, still is sitting, still is sitting</i><br />
<i>On the pallid bust of Pallas just above my chamber door;</i><br />
<i>And his eyes have all the seeming of a demon’s that is dreaming,</i><br />
<i>And the lamp-light o’er him streaming throws his shadow on the floor;</i><br />
<i>And my soul from out that shadow that lies floating on the floor</i><br />
<i>Shall be lifted — nevermore!</i>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_minor_songs_2">Other minor songs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li>Lenore Dove sung a song called "The Capitol Store," and is mentioned as having played "The Hanging Tree"<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>35<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>  for <a href="/wiki/Burdock_Everdeen" title="Burdock Everdeen">Burdock Everdeen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>36<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup></li>
<li>"Nothing You Can Take From Me" was alluded to as a song Lenore Dove would sing. Maysilee knew the titular phrase, but had never heard the song.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_34-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-34"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>34<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup></li>
<li>While trapped alone in the apartment after his victory, Haymitch has no control over the television. He is forced to listen to replays of gory Hunger Games deaths. However, one evening, a recording of Lucy Gray's performance of "The Old Therebefore" plays, to mess with Haymitch's mind.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>37<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Burdock sang the song at the funeral of <a href="/wiki/Willamae_Abernathy" title="Willamae Abernathy">Willamae</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sid_Abernathy" title="Sid Abernathy">Sid Abernathy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Louella_McCoy" title="Louella McCoy">Louella McCoy</a>, Maysilee Donner, and <a href="/wiki/Wyatt_Callow" title="Wyatt Callow">Wyatt</a> and <a href="/wiki/Jethro_Callow" title="Jethro Callow">Jethro Callow</a>'s funeral, comforting Haymitch.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>38<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Trivia">Trivia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>In <i>The Hunger Games</i> film, Katniss sings the first verse of "Deep in the Meadow" to Prim to calm her down from a nightmare, just before she leaves to go hunting on the morning of the Reaping.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG-39"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>39<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup></li>
<li>During training for the <a href="/wiki/75th_Hunger_Games" title="75th Hunger Games">75th Hunger Games</a>, Beetee mentions to Katniss that he has created a music chip small enough to be concealed in a flake of glitter, but that can hold hours of songs<sup id="cite_ref-CF_16_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF_16-40"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>40<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>.</li>
<li>While confined in the <a href="/wiki/Training_Center" title="Training Center">Training Center</a> following her assassination of <a href="/wiki/President_Coin" class="mw-redirect" title="President Coin">President Coin</a>, Katniss finds herself singing all the songs she had learned from her father, "Hour after hour of ballads, love songs, mountain airs."<sup id="cite_ref-MJ_27_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MJ_27-41"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>41<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup></li>
<li>Jennifer Lawrence was allegedly "horrified to sing" the Hanging Tree song. It was reported that she "cried a little bit in the morning before" the scene was shot. She was very insecure about her voice.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>42<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Fortunately, she did an amazing job and the song went to Number 1 worldwide on the iTunes Sales chart.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>43<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup></li></ul>
