</p><p><b>Tim O'Brien</b> is an American artist who illustrated the covers of <a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games_trilogy" title="The Hunger Games trilogy"><i>The Hunger Games</i> trilogy</a>, <i><a href="/wiki/The_Ballad_of_Songbirds_and_Snakes" title="The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes">The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</a></i>, and <i><a href="/wiki/Sunrise_on_the_Reaping" title="Sunrise on the Reaping">Sunrise on the Reaping</a></i>, as well as the alternate <a href="/wiki/Mockingjay" title="Mockingjay">mockingjay</a> design for the <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games" title="The Hunger Games">The Hunger Games</a></i> Collector's Edition.
</p><p>His illustrations have appeared in several magazines, including <i><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine)" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:Time (magazine)">Time</a></i>, <i><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:Newsweek">Newsweek</a></i>, and <i><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:Der Spiegel">Der Spiegel</a></i>. He has also designed several <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/postage_stamps" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:postage stamps">postage stamps</a> for the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:United States Postal Service">US Postal Service</a>. O'Brien is an <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professors_in_the_United_States#Adjunct_professor" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:Professors in the United States">adjunct professor</a> in <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_Communications_Design" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:Undergraduate Communications Design">Undergraduate Communications Design</a> at the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_Institute" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:Pratt Institute">Pratt Institute</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Hunger_Games"><i>The Hunger Games</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FTim_O%2527Brien%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D1&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Although characterizing himself as a "traditional painter," O'Brien decided to do the designs digitally, which helped out due to the trilogy's popularity.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> He is married to Elizabeth Parisi, Creative Director for Hardcovers at <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholastic_Corporation" class="extiw" title="wikipedia:Scholastic Corporation">Scholastic</a>, who was responsible for the cover designs. She has characterized the creation of the iconic <a href="/wiki/Mockingjay_pin" title="Mockingjay pin">mockingjay pin</a> as a collaboration. According to O'Brien, "It is not just a picture of a bird. It is something vulnerable. It is a symbol of the main character, <a href="/wiki/Katniss_Everdeen" title="Katniss Everdeen">Katniss</a>, who is tough and beautiful at the same time." It really says a lot about the heroine.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Before the final version was adopted, he had submitted a design for an arrow and an alternate version of the logo that had the mockingjay's right wing pierced with a chain. It could have meant that for the first and second books, Katniss is under the power of the Capitol; she is being chained to their power.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The process of designing the cover for <i><a href="/wiki/Catching_Fire" title="Catching Fire">Catching Fire</a></i> did not take as long, although O'Brien created several versions of the mockingjay in flight. The circle in the background was first conceived as a clock, then a ring of <a href="/wiki/Fire" title="Fire">fire</a>, before it evolved into a target.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
