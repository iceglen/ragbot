<p><b>Seneca Crane</b> was the <a href="/wiki/Head_Gamemaker" class="mw-redirect" title="Head Gamemaker">Head Gamemaker</a> of the <a href="/wiki/List_of_Hunger_Games" title="List of Hunger Games">72nd</a>, <a href="/wiki/73rd_Hunger_Games" title="73rd Hunger Games">73rd</a>, and <a href="/wiki/74th_Hunger_Games" title="74th Hunger Games">74th</a> <a href="/wiki/Hunger_Games_(event)" title="Hunger Games (event)">Hunger Games</a>, in charge of coordinating game play and ordering the creation of obstacles into the <a href="/wiki/Arena" title="Arena">arena</a>. At the end of the 74th Games, he allowed both <a href="/wiki/Katniss_Everdeen" title="Katniss Everdeen">Katniss Everdeen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Peeta_Mellark" title="Peeta Mellark">Peeta Mellark</a> to emerge as <a href="/wiki/Victors" class="mw-redirect" title="Victors">victors</a>, rather than allow them to commit suicide and thus have no victor at all.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG-1"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup> Unfortunately, this decision did not sit well with <a href="/wiki/President_Snow" class="mw-redirect" title="President Snow">President Snow</a>, and Crane was dead by the time of Katniss and Peeta's <a href="/wiki/Victory_Tour" title="Victory Tour">Victory Tour</a> six months later.
</p><p>He was possibly related to <a href="/wiki/Arachne_Crane" title="Arachne Crane">Arachne Crane</a>, who served as a <a href="/wiki/Mentor" title="Mentor">mentor</a> during the <a href="/wiki/10th_Hunger_Games" title="10th Hunger Games">10th Hunger Games</a>.
</p>

<p>Prior to the <a href="/wiki/Reaping" title="Reaping">Reaping</a>, Crane was interviewed by <a href="/wiki/Caesar_Flickerman" title="Caesar Flickerman">Caesar Flickerman</a> about his previous experiences as Head Gamemaker, and what defined his individual style of gameplay. He was also interviewed prior to the <a href="/wiki/Chariot_rides" title="Chariot rides">Tribute Parade</a> for his opinion of the "<a href="/wiki/Tribute" title="Tribute">recruits</a>" (as Caesar called them): While lacking a fully formed opinion at that stage, Crane acknowledged that Katniss Everdeen's unexpected volunteering created an interesting turn of events, which could affect gameplay later on.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG-1"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>
</p><p>Following the tributes' individual training sessions, Crane met with President Snow to discuss Katniss Everdeen's actions and the potential problems she could cause if such actions continued: Snow questioned the basis for her receiving a training score of 11- shooting an arrow at the Gamemakers (even though Katniss was actually aiming at the apple in the mouth of a roast pig); what Crane saw as a display of courage Snow interpreted as defiance, and he cautioned Crane that such actions could inspire hope - the only thing stronger than fear - and must be contained.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG-1"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>
</p>

<p>As Katniss' popularity continued to rise over the course of the games, so did her influence; an on-camera salute to the people of District 11 caused them to break out in rioting. Crane met with <a href="/wiki/Haymitch_Abernathy" title="Haymitch Abernathy">Haymitch Abernathy</a> for advice, and to convey President Snow's need to keep Katniss (and the <a href="/wiki/Districts" class="mw-redirect" title="Districts">districts</a>) in line: This led to the initial rule change that allowed two <a href="/wiki/Victors" class="mw-redirect" title="Victors">victors</a>, though Snow expressed his opposition to such indulgence.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG-1"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>

<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FSeneca_Crane%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D3&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure class="thumb  show-info-icon" typeof="mw:File/Thumb" style="width: 180px"> 	<a href="https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/thehungergames/images/a/a1/Seneca_Crane_berries_behind_the_scenes.JPG/revision/latest?cb=20231227191838" class="mw-file-description image"><img alt="Seneca locked in the room with a bowl of nightlock berries." src="data:image/gif;base64,R0lGODlhAQABAIABAAAAAP///yH5BAEAAAEALAAAAAABAAEAQAICTAEAOw%3D%3D" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="180" height="120" class="thumbimage lazyload" data-image-name="Seneca Crane berries behind the scenes.JPG" data-image-key="Seneca_Crane_berries_behind_the_scenes.JPG" data-relevant="1" data-src="https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/thehungergames/images/a/a1/Seneca_Crane_berries_behind_the_scenes.JPG/revision/latest/scale-to-width-down/180?cb=20231227191838"></a> 	<noscript><a href="https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/thehungergames/images/a/a1/Seneca_Crane_berries_behind_the_scenes.JPG/revision/latest?cb=20231227191838" class="mw-file-description image"><img alt="Seneca locked in the room with a bowl of nightlock berries." src="https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/thehungergames/images/a/a1/Seneca_Crane_berries_behind_the_scenes.JPG/revision/latest/scale-to-width-down/180?cb=20231227191838" decoding="async" loading="lazy" width="180" height="120" class="thumbimage" data-image-name="Seneca Crane berries behind the scenes.JPG" data-image-key="Seneca_Crane_berries_behind_the_scenes.JPG" data-relevant="1" data-src="https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/thehungergames/images/a/a1/Seneca_Crane_berries_behind_the_scenes.JPG/revision/latest/scale-to-width-down/180?cb=20231227191838"></a></noscript> 	<figcaption class="thumbcaption"> 		 			<a href="/wiki/File:Seneca_Crane_berries_behind_the_scenes.JPG" class="info-icon"><svg><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-info-small"></use></svg></a> 		 		 		 			<p class="caption">Seneca locked in the room with a bowl of nightlock berries.</p> 		 	</figcaption> </figure>
<p>After Katniss and Peeta threatened to consume poisonous <a href="/wiki/Nightlock" title="Nightlock">nightlock</a> berries and leave the Games with no victor at all, Crane spared their lives and allowed both to emerge as victors, despite President Snow's opposition<sup id="cite_ref-THG_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG-1"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>. In Snow's eyes, this act was the final straw and essentially marked the Head Gamemaker for death. In the book, it is never revealed exactly how Crane died, only that his death was ordered by Snow<sup id="cite_ref-CF_2_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF_2-2"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CF_17_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF_17-3"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>. In the <a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games_(film)" title="The Hunger Games (film)">film</a>, Snow has <a href="/wiki/Peacekeeper" class="mw-redirect" title="Peacekeeper">Peacekeepers</a> escort Crane to a room containing only a bowl of nightlock berries and lock him inside.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG-1"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup> It is likely that he committed suicide by eating some of the nightlock; In the film <i><a href="/wiki/Catching_Fire_(film)" class="mw-redirect" title="Catching Fire (film)">Catching Fire</a></i>, Plutarch mentioned to Katniss that Crane decided to "quit breathing... that or it was poison berries" implying that he ate the berries and died as a result<sup id="cite_ref-CF_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF-4"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FSeneca_Crane%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D4&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>As a result of his actions, Seneca inadvertently paved the way for the <a href="/wiki/Second_Rebellion" title="Second Rebellion">rebellion</a> that began in <i><a href="/wiki/Catching_Fire" title="Catching Fire">Catching Fire</a></i>. Had he killed both Katniss and Peeta, the idea of rebellion would have died along with the two tributes. While his granting mercy was likely an effort to save face more than anything, it revealed an unexpected chink in the Capitol's armor; the districts now realized that despite its power, the Capitol could be made to look foolish, and its agenda undermined. Katniss being an "underdog" only lent further weight to her successful defiance. More to the point, Crane's actions ruined one of the primary purposes of the Hunger Games - they were supposed to be a reminder to the districts that the Capitol controlled their very existence and could just as easily have had 24 people rounded up and executed at random, without giving them a chance to fight for their lives. Crane's actions would later be repeated by <a href="/wiki/Plutarch_Heavensbee" title="Plutarch Heavensbee">Plutarch Heavensbee</a>, who effectively collapsed the tradition of the Hunger Games when he secretly devised a plan to rescue Katniss from the Arena<sup id="cite_ref-CF_27_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF_27-5"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Catching_Fire"><i>Catching Fire</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FSeneca_Crane%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D5&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Seneca is first mentioned by name when President Snow revealed to Katniss that Crane was executed for not killing Katniss and Peeta when he had the chance.<sup id="cite_ref-CF_2_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF_2-2"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>
</p>

<p>During her individual session with the Gamemakers, Katniss is trying to find some way to display her defiance. She ends up grabbing a dummy, hanging it from a noose, and writing the name <i>SENECA CRANE</i> across it with some dark red berry mixture from the camouflage station.<sup id="cite_ref-CF_16_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF_16-6"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup> The Gamemakers are shocked and horrified with her performance, and quickly dismiss her.
</p><p>After Katniss tells <a href="/wiki/Effie_Trinket" title="Effie Trinket">Effie</a>, Haymitch, and Peeta about her performance, Effie says, "Oh Katniss, how do you even know about that?", and she replies "Is it a secret? President Snow didn't act like it was. In fact, he seemed eager for me to know."<sup id="cite_ref-CF_17_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF_17-3"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup> This could mean several things; that the news of Crane's death was not widely circulated, that people didn't know President Snow was behind it, or simply that they believed whatever story had been publicized about his disappearance (the novels give no specific indication).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Personality_and_traits">Personality and traits</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FSeneca_Crane%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D6&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As the Head Gamemaker of the Hunger Games, Seneca Crane was a bloodthirsty, cruel individual. He openly showed pleasure in creating various dangers in the arena for the tributes, such as mutts, as well as watching the tributes kill one another. He seemed to like his job more for the fun and drama of it, not for the murder; this was consistent with the behavior of many in the Capitol population, who tended to view the Hunger Games as more of a reality show than an actual death game. However, unlike President Snow and many of his associates, Crane was not completely corrupted and showed a degree of mercy by allowing both Katniss Everdeen and Peeta Mellark to survive the Hunger Games. 
</p><p>In the film, he seemed uncomfortable when interacting with President Snow, and he did not fully agree with the Capitol's harsh regime. However, it was not until the double suicide threat made by Everdeen and Mellark that he finally decided to not go along with everything Snow said. This ultimately led to his execution.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG-1"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Physical_description">Physical description</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FSeneca_Crane%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D7&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games_(film)" title="The Hunger Games (film)"><i>The Hunger Games</i> film</a>, Crane is portrayed with pale skin, light blueish-gray eyes, and dark hair. He sports a uniquely styled beard, shaved in an intricate design, and his hair appears to be heavily styled with product. The film also mentioned that the 74th Hunger Games marks Crane's third year as Head Gamemaker.<sup id="cite_ref-THG_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-THG-1"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Etymology">Etymology</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FSeneca_Crane%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D8&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In Roman history, Seneca the Younger, also known as Lucius Annaeus Seneca, or often just Seneca; a philosopher and playwright. He was well known in Roman society. He held a seat in the Senate, but later he was exiled. Like Seneca Crane, he was also forced to commit suicide.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Trivia">Trivia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FSeneca_Crane%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D9&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>In a rough draft of the screenplay for the 2012 film adaptation, Seneca had a much larger role than in the finished film. His dialogue was greatly expanded, giving a glimpse of his personality outside of his role as Head Gamemaker.
<ul><li>In the screenplay's final scene, President Snow and Seneca were aboard a hovercraft idling over the arena. Snow rebuked Seneca for allowing Katniss and Peeta to live, then opened a box revealing a bowl of nightlock berries. Seneca shook his head, silently declining, and Snow replied, "Good luck, Seneca. And may the odds be ever in your favor!" Seneca was then thrown out of the hovercraft, falling to his death.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7"><span class="cite-bracket">[</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">]</span></a></sup></li></ul></li>
<li>He was most likely a relative of <a href="/wiki/Arachne_Crane" title="Arachne Crane">Arachne Crane</a> because they share the same last name.</li></ul>
