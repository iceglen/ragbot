<dd>&#8212;<a href="/wiki/Lucy_Gray_Baird" title="Lucy Gray Baird">Lucy Gray Baird</a> to <a href="/wiki/Coriolanus_Snow" title="Coriolanus Snow">Coriolanus Snow</a></dd></dl></div>
<p><i><b>The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</b></i> is a YA prequel novel to the dystopian fictional world of <a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games_trilogy" title="The Hunger Games trilogy"><i>The Hunger Games</i> trilogy</a> written by American author <a href="/wiki/Suzanne_Collins" title="Suzanne Collins">Suzanne Collins</a>. Its premise is set 64 years prior to the events explored in the first book of the trilogy, 2008's bestselling <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games" title="The Hunger Games">The Hunger Games</a>.</i> The prequel is stylistically written in the third-person point of view, diverging from the original trilogy, which is told using the first-person point of view of protagonist <a href="/wiki/Katniss_Everdeen" title="Katniss Everdeen">Katniss Everdeen</a>.
</p><p>The book was announced on June 16, 2019 by <i>Scholastic Press</i>. Suzanne Collins said in a statement that she would return to the world of <a href="/wiki/Panem" title="Panem">Panem</a>, the setting of the original trilogy, and explore the time period after the districts' failed rebellion in the <a href="/wiki/Dark_Days" title="Dark Days">Dark Days</a>, referred to as the <a href="/wiki/Dark_Days" title="Dark Days">Dark Days</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The cover art and title was announced by <i>Scholastic</i> at <i>New York Comic Con</i> on October 4, 2019. The cover art for the novel was designed by Tim O'Brien.
</p><p>The main character was revealed to be a young <a href="/wiki/Coriolanus_Snow" title="Coriolanus Snow">Coriolanus Snow</a> on January 21, 2020, the same day an excerpt from the novel was released.<sup id="cite_ref-Entertainment_Weekly_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Entertainment_Weekly-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Premise">Premise</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FThe_Ballad_of_Songbirds_and_Snakes%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D1&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>The novel follows the journey of an 18-year-old Coriolanus Snow and is set in the world of Panem 64 years before the events of the original <i>Hunger Games</i> trilogy. The book starts on the morning of the <a href="/wiki/Reaping" title="Reaping">reaping</a> for the <a href="/wiki/10th_Hunger_Games" title="10th Hunger Games">10th Hunger Games</a>—well before the lifetime of the original trilogy's protagonist Katniss Everdeen. Historically, the book explores the reconstruction period of Panem, 10 years after the Dark Days. With the backdrop of the Games looming, the aftermath of a war torn country struggling back to its feet, and power up for grabs, there is plenty of fertile ground for characters to grapple with questions of morality, define their views of humanity, and comprehend exactly what it takes to ignore them.
</p>

</p><p><i>It is the morning of the reaping that will kick off the tenth annual Hunger Games. In <a href="/wiki/The_Capitol" title="The Capitol">the Capitol</a>, eighteen-year-old Coriolanus Snow is preparing for his one shot at glory as a <a href="/wiki/Mentor" title="Mentor">mentor</a> in the Games. The once-mighty house of Snow has fallen on hard times, its fate hanging on the slender chance that Coriolanus will be able to outcharm, outwit, and outmaneuver his fellow students to mentor the winning tribute.</i><br />
</p><p><i>The odds are against him. He’s been given the humiliating assignment of mentoring the female <a href="/wiki/Tribute" title="Tribute">tribute</a> from <a href="/wiki/District_12" title="District 12">District 12</a>, the lowest of the low. Their fates are now completely intertwined — every choice Coriolanus makes could lead to favor or failure, triumph or ruin. Inside the <a href="/wiki/Arena" title="Arena">arena</a>, it will be a fight to the death. Outside the arena, Coriolanus starts to feel for his doomed tribute . . . and must weigh his need to follow the rules against his desire to survive no matter what it takes.</i><sup id="cite_ref-Amazon_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amazon-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Plot_summary">Plot summary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FThe_Ballad_of_Songbirds_and_Snakes%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D3&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div style="border:4px double #ffe600; border-radius:1em;"><center><i>Main article: <a href="/wiki/The_Ballad_of_Songbirds_and_Snakes/Summary" title="The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes/Summary">The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes/Summary</a></i></center></div>
<p>Coriolanus Snow is one of 24 students of the <a href="/wiki/Academy" title="Academy">Academy</a> selected to <a href="/wiki/Mentor" title="Mentor">mentor</a> a tribute in the 10th Hunger Games. His family has fallen on hard times and he is assigned the female tribute from District 12, a girl named <a href="/wiki/Lucy_Gray_Baird" title="Lucy Gray Baird">Lucy Gray Baird</a> of a singing and performance group called the <a href="/wiki/Covey" title="Covey">Covey</a>. He quickly forms a bond with the girl, but things get chaotic before the games even begin. One of the chosen mentors, Arachne Crane, is killed by her own tribute and several tributes and mentors are injured and killed in a bombing of the <a href="/wiki/Capitol_Arena" title="Capitol Arena">Capitol Arena</a>.
</p><p>Coriolanus Snow tries everything he can to help his tribute, but is also drawn into the troubles of his classmate, <a href="/wiki/Sejanus_Plinth" title="Sejanus Plinth">Sejanus Plinth</a>. Sejanus is originally from <a href="/wiki/District_2" title="District 2">District 2</a> and is horrified by the treatment of the tributes and particular his own tribute, <a href="/wiki/Marcus" title="Marcus">Marcus</a>, who is strung up in the arena for trying to escape during the bombing of the arena. Coriolanus ends up in the Arena himself, at the orders of Dr. <a href="/wiki/Volumnia_Gaul" title="Volumnia Gaul">Volumnia Gaul</a>, the Head <a href="/wiki/Gamemaker" title="Gamemaker">Gamemaker</a>, who has taken a personal interest in Coriolanus. He rescues Sejanus, but ends up killing one of the tributes, <a href="/wiki/Bobbin" title="Bobbin">Bobbin</a>, in the process. As the Games continue, he ends up taking more desperate measures when he learns that Dr. Gaul is releasing <a href="/wiki/Snake_mutt" title="Snake mutt">snake mutts</a> in the arena. He places a handkerchief riddled with Lucy Gray's scent in the snakes' tank to prevent them from attacking her. This does work, and the snakes seem mesmerised when Lucy Gray begins to sing “The Old Therebefore”. Coriolanus wonders whether this magical scene was happening because of Lucy Gray’s singing, or the fact that in an open, vast arena, her scent was one the snakes were familiar with. Lucy Gray then makes it to the final three, where she kills Treech by throwing one of the snake muttations that she had hidden in her dress, at him. She then kills Reaper by exhausting him in the searing sun, and poisoning him. Lucy Gray then waits for over half an hour, until she is finally declared the winner of the 10th Hunger Games. However, shortly afterward, Coriolanus is confronted with his father‘s handkerchief that he’d dropped in the muttation tank, a napkin he used to bring food to Lucy Gray, and his mother's <a href="/wiki/Silver_compact" title="Silver compact">compact</a>, filled with the rat poison he instructed Lucy Gray to use by the Academy's dean, <a href="/wiki/Casca_Highbottom" title="Casca Highbottom">Casca Highbottom</a>, who seems to have some sort of grudge against him. Highbottom expels him from the Academy, but promises not to reveal his disgrace if he enrolls in the <a href="/wiki/Peacekeepers" title="Peacekeepers">Peacekeepers</a>.
</p><p>Coriolanus enrolls in the Peacekeepers, asking to be assigned to District 12, where he has fallen head-over-heels for Lucy Gray Baird, though he worries about her spurned lover, <a href="/wiki/Billy_Taupe" class="mw-redirect" title="Billy Taupe">Billy Taupe</a>. In District 12, things quickly become tangled, as Sejanus Plinth was made to enroll in the Peacekeepers as well. Sejanus seems bent on helping the rebels in District 12. Coriolanus learns of the bubbling rebel resentment in the community of District 12, while trying to form a new life with Lucy Gray as well. He also takes the test to become an officer with the Peacekeepers, Sejanus having used his father <a href="/wiki/Strabo_Plinth" title="Strabo Plinth">Strabo Plinth</a>'s influence to secure a diploma for Coriolanus. Things come to a head when Coriolanus discovers Sejanus aiding the rebels. He covertly informs the Capitol of the activity, but he himself ends up shooting <a href="/wiki/Mayfair_Lipp" title="Mayfair Lipp">Mayfair Lipp</a>, the mayor's daughter and Billy Taupe's other lover. He hatches a desperate plan with Lucy Gray to run away north, before learning that he passed the officer's test.
</p><p>Worried that he will still be implicated in the crime, Coriolanus runs away with Lucy Gray anyway, but then discovers that he can get rid of the evidence. At the same time, Lucy Gray realizes his betrayal of Sejanus and his loyalty to the Capitol. She runs away and sets a trap for him. He tries to track her down, but is bitten by a snake in the woods. He fires into the woods, hoping to kill her, though it is unclear if his shots connect. The next day, he travels away to become an officer, but is diverted back to the Capitol by Dr. Gaul. She has decided to take him on as her protege. He begins studying at the <a href="/wiki/University" title="University">University</a> in the Capitol and also takes on a position as an apprentice Gamemaker although the others treat him as a full fledged member. Coriolanus then implemented many of his ideas into the Games that we see in later years. The Plinths fund his studies and lifestyle, convinced he was a great friend of their son and unaware of his betrayal. He poisons Casca Highbottom, the first victim of his signature method of dispatching his enemies, reflecting that "Snow lands on top."
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Main_characters">Main characters</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a title="Sign in to edit" class="mw-editsection-visualeditor" data-tracking-label="log-in-edit-section" data-action="edit-section" href="https://auth.fandom.com/signin?redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fthehungergames.fandom.com%2Fwiki%2FThe_Ballad_of_Songbirds_and_Snakes%3Fveaction%3Dedit%26section%3D4&amp;uselang=en" data-testid="log-in-edit-section"><svg class="wds-icon wds-icon-tiny"><use xlink:href="#wds-icons-pencil-tiny"></use></svg></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li><b><a href="/wiki/Coriolanus_Snow" title="Coriolanus Snow">Coriolanus Snow</a></b> - One of the 24 mentors assigned by the Academy for the 10th Hunger Games. His family was once one of power in the Capitol, but has since fallen on hard times. He is assigned to mentor <a href="/wiki/Lucy_Gray_Baird" title="Lucy Gray Baird">Lucy Gray Baird</a>, the female tribute from District 12. He becomes embroiled in the ongoing conflict between the Capitol and rebels and must face matters of love, chaos, contract and control.</li>
<li><b><a href="/wiki/Lucy_Gray_Baird" title="Lucy Gray Baird">Lucy Gray Baird</a></b> - The mentee of Coriolanus Snow and the female tribute from District 12. A talented singer and part of a group known as the <a href="/wiki/Covey" title="Covey">Covey</a>, she makes a dramatic entrance into the Hunger Games and later becomes District 12's very first victor. Lucy Gray and Coriolanus fall in love, but upon finding out who he really is, Lucy Gray attempts to run away, setting a trap for him. Her ending mirrors the poem by William Wordsworth for which she was named.</li>
<li><b><a href="/wiki/Casca_Highbottom" title="Casca Highbottom">Casca Highbottom</a></b> - The dean of the Academy and credited for creating the Games. Doped up on <a href="/wiki/Morphling_(drug)" title="Morphling (drug)">morphling</a>, he has a dark secret in his past and seems to bear a grudge against Coriolanus Snow.</li>
<li><b><a href="/wiki/Sejanus_Plinth" title="Sejanus Plinth">Sejanus Plinth</a></b> - The mentor for the <a href="/wiki/District_2" title="District 2">District 2</a> male tribute. He is from District 2 himself and still sympathizes with those in the districts. His father, <a href="/wiki/Strabo_Plinth" title="Strabo Plinth">Strabo Plinth</a>, is a powerful war profiteer who bought his family a new life in the Capitol, but struggles against his son's rebellious ways.</li>
<li><b><a href="/wiki/Clemensia_Dovecote" title="Clemensia Dovecote">Clemensia Dovecote</a></b> - The mentor for the <a href="/wiki/District_11" title="District 11">District 11</a> male tribute. She is the daughter of the Capitol's energy secretary.</li>
<li><b><a href="/wiki/Tigris" title="Tigris">Tigris</a></b> - As Coriolanus's cousin, she has lived with the Snow family from a very young age. She and Coriolanus are more like close siblings, and she worries over him as the family's financial situation worsens and the fires of the Hunger Games ignite.</li>
<li><b><a href="/wiki/Grandma%27am" title="Grandma&#39;am">Grandma'am</a></b> - The grandmother of Coriolanus Snow. A regal woman, she sings <a href="/wiki/The_Capitol" title="The Capitol">the Capitol</a> anthem "<a href="/wiki/Gem_of_Panem" title="Gem of Panem">Gem of Panem</a>" and grows roses in a rooftop garden. She dotes on Coriolanus and often speaks of the Snow family grandeur. However, as the Snows struggle to mask their poor financial situation, she begins to descend into denial and eventually despair.</li>
<li><b><a href="/wiki/Volumnia_Gaul" title="Volumnia Gaul">Volumnia Gaul</a></b> - The Head <a href="/wiki/Gamemaker" title="Gamemaker">Gamemaker</a> of the 10th Hunger Games. She works in the Capitol's experimental weapons division at the <a href="/wiki/Citadel" title="Citadel">Citadel</a> and is also a top professor at the Capitol's <a href="/wiki/University" title="University">University</a>. She believes that people are inherently violent and is convinced that war is eternal and must be managed.</li>
<li><b><a href="/wiki/Billy_Taupe_Clade" title="Billy Taupe Clade">Billy Taupe</a></b> - Lucy Gray Baird's old boyfriend. He ditches Lucy Gray for a much richer <a href="/wiki/Mayfair_Lipp" title="Mayfair Lipp">Mayfair Lipp</a>, and upon learning that Billy Taupe was dating them both, Mayfair ordered her father, the mayor, to call out Lucy Gray's name in the reaping.</li></ul>

<p>On April 21, 2020, the film release was announced being officially in development. <a href="/wiki/Francis_Lawrence" title="Francis Lawrence">Francis Lawrence</a>, director of <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Catching_Fire" title="The Hunger Games: Catching Fire">The Hunger Games: Catching Fire</a></i> as well as both <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Mockingjay_-_Part_1" title="The Hunger Games: Mockingjay - Part 1">The Hunger Games: Mockingjay - Part 1</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Mockingjay_-_Part_2" title="The Hunger Games: Mockingjay - Part 2">Part 2</a></i>, was confirmed to return as director. <a href="/wiki/Nina_Jacobson" title="Nina Jacobson">Nina Jacobson</a> returned as a producer for the film alongside author Suzanne Collins. <p>Regarding the new title, Scholastic publisher and editorial director David Levithan stated "<i>Over a decade ago, Panem provided a very useful lens through which to see our own world, and I think applies as well in 2020, where questions of power and moral choice are as prevalent as ever. It’s also fascinating to revisit a place you feel you know, but 64 years earlier.</i>" Author Suzanne Collins stated that "<i>With this book, I wanted to explore the state of nature, who we are, and what we perceive is required for our survival.</i>"<sup id="cite_ref-Parade_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parade-7"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>

<p>The cover art for <i>The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</i> was designed by Tim O'Brien. It features a golden mockingjay at a new angle, entwined with a snake. Regarding the cover art, VP publisher and editorial director for Scholastic David Levithan stated that "<i>This cover does an extraordinary job of capturing the conflict—both inner and outer—that lies at the heart of </i>The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes<i>. The mockingjay has returned, but at a new angle . . . which is very much in line with the story that Suzanne Collins is telling.</i>"<sup id="cite_ref-Scholastic_Announces_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Scholastic_Announces-8"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>8<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>

Reviewer Seira Wilson of Amazon Book Review stated "It’s been a decade since <i><a href="/wiki/Mockingjay" title="Mockingjay">Mockingjay</a></i>, the final book in the Hunger Games trilogy, was published and fans are going to be thrilled with Suzanne Collins’ unexpected prequel, <i>The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</i>. This novel has been under serious lockdown, so all that can be said until May 19 is that it begins on the day of the reaping for the tenth annual Hunger Games, and an eighteen-year-old Coriolanus Snow is mentoring the underdog tribute from District 12. Prepare to read this in book one sitting because you won’t be able to put it down."<sup id="cite_ref-Amazon_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amazon-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>As of the evening of May 16, 2020, the title was #5 on Amazon's "Charts" feature, below John Grisham's <i>Camino Winds</i> and above Stephen King's <i>If It Bleeds</i><sup id="cite_ref-Amazon_Charts_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amazon_Charts-23"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>23<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> and had reached the status of being the company's #1 bestseller in books.<sup id="cite_ref-Amazon_Best_Sellers_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Amazon_Best_Sellers-24"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>24<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>Since its release on May 19, 2020, the title has received mostly positive reviews from critics. Sarah Lyall of <i>The New York Times</i> gave the book an overall positive review, but felt that its ending feels "flat and desultory" following the excitement of the earlier portions. Annalisa Quinn of NPR panned the novel overall, feeling that Coriolanus is ultimately a "flat, wily sociopath," whereas the character of <a href="/wiki/Katniss_Everdeen" title="Katniss Everdeen">Katniss Everdeen</a> allowed for contradiction and that while <i>The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes</i> tells us what we should think.<sup id="cite_ref-Book_Marks_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Book_Marks-25"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>25<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Constance Grady of Vox described the book as "your apocalyptic escape from our current apocalypse," noting that as the current world grows to feel more apocalyptic, "it can be oddly comforting to escape to a fictional dystopia."<sup id="cite_ref-Vox_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vox-26"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>26<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Karin Tanabe of the Washington Post gave particular praise to the third and final portion of the book, stating that it is "the most revelatory in terms of the gradual chipping away of Coryo’s humanity." She also praises the title for filling in the blanks regarding questions such as “Which unhinged savage came up with kids killing kids?” or “What’s the meaning behind the song ‘The Hanging Tree’?<sup id="cite_ref-Washington_Post_Review_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Washington_Post_Review-27"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>27<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The title sold more than 270,000 units in its first week of publication, resulting in a 77.4% jump in sales in the YA fiction category as compared with the previous year.<sup id="cite_ref-Publisher&#39;s_Weekly_Sales_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Publisher&#39;s_Weekly_Sales-28"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>28<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
