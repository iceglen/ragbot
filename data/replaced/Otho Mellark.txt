Otho Mellark was a baker. He was a large, broad-shouldered man with burn scars from years at the oven. He was not a talkative man, but he was described as being much nicer than his wife, and he was quite generous in his trades. He did not seem to harbor any resentment and treated children kindly and respectfully. He had a wife and three sons. He and his family lived above the bakery where they worked. After saying goodbye to his youngest son after the reaping, he visited another, gave her a packet of cookies, and promised to keep an eye on her sister and make sure she was eating. He presumably made this visit because of past feelings for the sister's mother, as well as the business relationship he had developed over the years. He and the rest of his family were killed when the district was bombed. He was included in a memory book made after the war, highlighting how he brought the cookies.
