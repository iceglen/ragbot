Food is a major theme in the narrative. This theme manifests in a variety of ways: descriptions of meals, whether simple or elaborate, are a regular feature; for several years, putting food on her family's table was the primary focus of a character's existence; and it was through food (specifically bread) that a connection was made that set many later events in motion.

On a more symbolic level, descriptions of food often help to set the tone for a particular scene: They help to distinguish between different areas of the setting, and reveal details about society and culture. In poorer areas, the people have a very hardscrabble existence, and the foods listed tend to be very basic and/or low in quality, such as bread made from ration grain and dishes made from whatever ingredients can be found in the surrounding woods. In contrast, cuisine in the capital is often described as very over-the-top, made with an endless variety of expensive and high quality ingredients, and served in large portions or buffets. Even within the capital, however, not everyone sets the same table, and certain characters struggle with basic food needs as much as anyone in the outlying districts. Aside from such distinctions, different types of food are associated with specific areas, depending of the district's industry, climate, and economic/demographic makeup.
In the Training Center, the tributes' rooms are equipped with machines that deliver freshly prepared food to the rooms' occupants in less than a minute.
