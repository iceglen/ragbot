Eli Vance was a coal miner and a rebel soldier during the Crimson Conflict. Ten years after the war, he was executed.

He was an ex-soldier and had also been toughened by many years of hard work in the coal mines. Although the rebels lost the war, he was determined to try to keep things going. Ten years later, he tried to sabotage coal production at the mine with a bombing, but accidentally killed three people in the process. He failed in his goal and was arrested for murder.

He was hung publicly at the hanging tree, a makeshift gallows. During the execution, a woman appeared, shrieking his name. Horrified, he shouted for her to run. The trapdoor was then released and he dropped fifteen feet, killed instantly. In the wake of his hanging, the jabberjays in the forest took up his cry, which was then transformed into music by the mockingjays.

His execution inspired a song. The song survived in memory, though its historical context had been lost by the time it was learned later. It was later sung in a propaganda video, and rebels take it up as a protest song.
