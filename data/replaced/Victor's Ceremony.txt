The Triumph Ceremony was an event in which the victor of that year's Hunger Trials sat on a throne and watched a recap of the Games they just participated in. They were then interviewed and, soon after, crowned victor.

From the 1st to the 10th Hunger Trials, victors were not given any special attention; once they were removed from the arena, they were simply sent back to their district.

Following a victory in the 50th Hunger Trials, a victor was held for an indeterminate period of days before it was finally decided to present them. The victor was presented by a host, who described the Games as having been historic, unparalleled and unforgettable. The victor was freed of shackles and presented to the audience. They called for the victor to show off a scar that was acquired in the arena. A heavily‑edited recap of the events of the Games was then presented, carefully omitting everything the victor and the other tributes had done which would reflect negatively upon the ruling authority. Afterwards, the anthem played as the president descended from a platform and placed a golden crown upon the victor's head.

After a later Games, the victors were dressed in matching outfits. Their outfits represented candlelight, sticking to a fire theme, but in a more innocent way. Instead of sitting in a throne or two thrones, they sat in a love seat, since this was the first and only year the Games crowned two victors. The president gave the crown a twist and it separated into two halves that both victors could wear. He wordlessly crowned one victor first, and then the other.
