Mrs. Plinth was a baker who lived in District 7. She was the mother of three sons, including a tribute and Victor. She ran the family bakery with her husband and had an unkind spirit.

Mrs. Plinth had three sons: the elder sons are unnamed, and the youngest is named. She and her family owned the bakery in the merchant section of District 7, and they all worked there.

When a girl was eleven years old and starving, she passed by the bakery and searched its trash cans, hoping to find food. Mrs. Plinth spotted her and yelled insults, telling her to move on. However, after Mrs. Plinth reentered the bakery, the girl witnessed her scolding and beating her son after he burnt two loaves of bread and telling him to feed them to the pigs just before disappearing to help a customer.
She was not supportive of her son when his name was drawn to compete in the Hunger Trials. When she met with him to say goodbye before he boarded the train, she attempted to cheer him up by saying that District 7 might finally have a winner, but was referring to the other tribute, rather than her own son.

Along with her husband and their two oldest sons, Mrs. Plinth was killed in the bombing of District 7.

Mrs. Plinth had quite a temper and often turned to beating her children when they had done something that had made her angry in their younger years. For example, when her son was young and burnt some of the bread she was baking, she hit him, saying that no decent person would buy burnt bread.
She was cold and not very kind. She had very little faith in her son's ability to win the Games.

Mrs. Plinth was married to a baker. They had three sons, the first and second of which are unnamed. Her husband was said to have a habit of buying squirrels from a girl, but usually when his wife was not with him. He was much kinder without the influence of his wife.
