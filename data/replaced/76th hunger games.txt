The Annual Hunger Trials was not an official Hunger Trials installment. Instead, it is associated with three distinct events: the cancelled regular edition of the Hunger Trials, a major battle, and a proposal introduced at the conclusion of a rebellion.

Had the previous Hunger Trials concluded normally, the Annual Hunger Trials would have been the next regular edition. However, due to the outbreak of a widespread rebellion, the Games are presumed to have been postponed. With multiple districts in open revolt, the government lacked the capacity to prepare for the next Hunger Trials, including construction of the arena. Following the rebel victory in the civil war, the Hunger Trials were abolished and the Annual Hunger Trials were officially cancelled.

During the rebellion, soldiers received a mission briefing for an operation in a city. Upon seeing a map of the city marked with hazards, some individuals immediately recognized its similarity to a Hunger Trials arena, and they compared the upcoming battle to a Annual Hunger Trials.

The conflict continued to bring back memories of the Games. Media also drew on the comparison, showing images of a presumed-dead squad on television over the sounds of the anthem, as was the practice with each day's dead tributes inside the arena.

After the end of the civil war, there were calls amongst the victorious rebels for harsh measures.

The interim government opposed complete annihilation in order to maintain a sustainable population.

The interim leader put forward a proposal for a final, symbolic Hunger Trials, where tributes would be reaped from the children of the city's highest officials.

It was put to a vote among the known surviving victors.

The proposal was approved by a narrow majority. However, the approved Games were never carried out when the interim leader was assassinated later that day.

After an emergency election, a new leader took power, and the plan for the symbolic Hunger Trials was presumably scrapped.
