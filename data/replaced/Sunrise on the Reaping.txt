The Hunger Games: Dawn of the Reaping is a prequel novel to a dystopian young adult trilogy. It is set twenty-four years before the events of the original trilogy, starting on the morning of the reaping for the 50th annual Hunger Games, an event known as the Second 25-Year Hunger Games. The story revisits a dystopian nation and the annual event where young people are chosen to fight to the death.

The narrative begins on the morning of the reaping, which is also the birthday of a teenage boy from an impoverished district. Tensions are high because it is a 25-Year Hunger Games, meaning twice the normal number of tributes will be chosen. The boy hopes to get through the ceremony and spend time with his girlfriend. During the reaping, one selected boy attempts to run and is killed. The boy intervenes in the aftermath and is consequently chosen as a replacement tribute.

The champions are taken to a central city. During a public parade, a disturbance causes a fatal accident with one of the tributes. The champions are assigned mentors. During training, the boy is attacked by another tribute. He later meets a man who reveals his own son is being forced to participate as punishment. The boy is then threatened by the nation's leader.

After the accidental death, a replacement tribute is introduced. The remaining tributes realize they may be able to use this replacement to feed false information to their captors. An alliance is formed to counter the dominant group of tributes. The boy receives the lowest possible training score. During a televised interview, he frames his low score as a virtue and emphasizes his alliance, though he secretly plans to separate from the group. Before the Games begin, he learns his girlfriend has been arrested but is unharmed. A cameraman reveals he is part of a plot to sabotage the arena.

As the Games begin, the boy avoids the initial violence, gathers supplies, and escapes into a wooded area. He discovers the local water and food are poisoned, and realizes the only safe supplies are those from the starting area. He decides his strategy will be to outlast the others by conserving his resources. He learns that half of the dominant alliance has been killed. The replacement tribute later dies from poisonous pollen. The boy hides the body as an act of defiance but is attacked by mutant creatures.

He meets the son of the man he previously met, and they discuss plans to bomb a section of the arena to cause a flood. They succeed, but in retaliation, the gamemakers send creatures that kill the son. In grief, the boy attempts to hack the arena's systems, causing a temporary malfunction. The gamemakers then cause a volcano to erupt, which he survives.

The boy plans to break out of the arena by heading north, which leads him into a hedge maze. After escaping, he is ambushed by three tributes from the dominant alliance. He kills two, but is disarmed by the third. As he is about to be killed, a female tribute from his district saves him using a poisoned dart. They decide to become allies.

The two allies are later ambushed by two other tributes. All four fall into an area where gamemakers are working. Two gamemakers are killed and a third dies in the fall. The boy and his ally part ways upon learning only five tributes remain. The boy discovers a property of the arena's force field. He hears his ally screaming and finds her attacked by mutant birds; she dies. He spends time with another tribute. The next day, he finds that tribute decapitated. He fights the final remaining tribute, who is killed by her own weapon rebounding off the force field.

With the final death, the boy is the winner. Still determined to make an impact, he prepares to detonate an explosive. He hears a voice ordering him to stop but ignores it and throws the explosive. He believes he is dying in the blast.

He wakes up confused to find himself alive, kept in solitary confinement. He is eventually taken for a victor's ceremony, where a highly edited version of events is presented, erasing all his attempts at sabotage. The nation's leader crowns him. He is sent back to his district with the bodies of his fallen allies. Upon arriving home, he finds his family home on fire with his mother and brother inside; he is restrained from entering. He attends their funeral and is directed to a special village for winners.

He goes to find his girlfriend, who had been under house arrest. They have a happy reunion, but she eats poisoned candy given to her as punishment and dies in his arms. Haunted, he isolates himself, drinks heavily, and withdraws from the world. He is later taken on a mandatory victory tour, where the cameraman again pitches the idea of rebellion, but the boy rejects him. He returns to his home, knowing his future will to mentor new tributes sent to their deaths each year.

An epilogue reflects on the boy seeing a young girl grow up in his district. He is initially reluctant but finally tells his story to her and another boy ..
