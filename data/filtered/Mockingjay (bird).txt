The mockingjay is a bird that was created through the unintended mating of mockingbirds and jabberjays. The father of the species was the jabberjay, an exclusively male muttation species that was invented to spy on rebels. Jabberjays could memorize entire conversations and repeat them back to their handlers. However, rebels caught on and fed lies to the birds. After that, the operation was shut down and jabberjays were released into the wild in the hope that they would die off. They eventually did, but not before mating with female mockingbirds, producing the mockingjay. This was unforeseen, because no one expected jabberjays to be able to reproduce with other bird species.

Mockingjays are black and glossy crested birds with white patches on the undersides of their wings. They are slightly larger than jabberjays. Like jabberjays, they are excellent mimics, and they're known for their ability to memorize and repeat both birdsong and human voices. Although mockingjays can't enunciate words, they can echo any vocalization. They can even whistle the melodies of songs with multiple verses. They pick up tunes quickly and spread them to other nearby mockingjays. Supposedly, they will only repeat songs if they enjoy the singer's voice.

Mockingjays have a certain level of symbolism. The birds became a widespread symbol of rebellion. Wearing a mockingjay symbol in any form is considered a sign of rebellion. For some, the bird and pin could symbolize remembrance. For others, the birds are a reminder of home and family. Mockingjays have several distinctive meanings for citizens of the ruling power. On the one hand, they are a reminder of past mistakes, of the tricks played by the rebels and the failure in killing off their own creation of jabberjays. On the other hand, images of mockingjays became a fashion trend.
