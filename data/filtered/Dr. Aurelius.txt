Dr. Aurelius is a doctor who specialized in mental health care during the period of upheaval around the Second Rebellion.

Dr. Aurelius was a head doctor. Directly after the end of the war, he took on a client during a stay in the Capitol. Other doctors were confused when the client didn't talk, and tests were conducted on the vocal cords, showing an ability to speak. He came up with the theory that the client had become a "mental, rather than physical, Avox," from emotional trauma. He instructed the other doctors to leave the client alone. The client liked him because he didn't offer empty assurances; he just asked if the client would like to talk, and when there was no response, he used their sessions as opportunities to take naps.

After a significant event, Dr. Aurelius was summoned to a trial, where he presented the client as a "hopeless, shell-shocked lunatic" to save from a criminal conviction. In the end, the client was confined, and was supposed to keep in contact with Dr. Aurelius to monitor mental condition, although contact was not maintained.

Dr. Aurelius treated another character and later cleared that character to return. A message was conveyed that he can't pretend to be treating the client forever, and contact was eventually made. Dr. Aurelius' advice was to just go through the motions, and the client told him an idea about making a book of memories. Dr. Aurelius agreed and sent a large box of parchment paper to help with the project.

In a film adaptation, Dr. Aurelius made a brief appearance as a rebel doctor. This iteration of the doctor was a female medical doctor who treated a character after a physical attack.
