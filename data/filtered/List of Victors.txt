A list of Hunger Games victors.

Known victors
There have been 75 victors throughout the history of the Hunger Games: one for each year from the 1st through 73rd, and then an unprecedented double victory in the 74th Hunger Games. No victor was crowned for the 75th Hunger Games. All victors were between the ages of 14 and 18 at the time of their victory.
At the time of the reaping for the 75th Hunger Games, 59 victors were still alive, and one victor's fate is unknown, so fifteen died prior to the third Quarter Quell. The Career districts had the most victors, but each district had at least one surviving male and female. Eighteen more died in the Quell, and by the end of the Second Rebellion, only seven victors were shown alive. This means that another 34 presumably died during the Victors' Purge and the rebellion.
Only 27 victors were mentioned in the trilogy. Two more were revealed by a promotional website, and another appeared in a prequel, for a total of 30 known victors. The table below lists the 23 named victors.

Unknown victors
Since there have been 75 victors in the history of the Hunger Games and 31 are known, there are still 44 unknown victors.

Books
From the 1st-9th Hunger Games, two districts already produced more victors than other districts. Two others were also considered contenders, so they may have turned out victors as well, or at least had tributes place highly.
It is stated that one district does the worst in the games, with just two victors. By this, it can be assumed that every other district has at least three victors by the seventy-fourth Hunger Games.
When one character was "little", a district had a male victor who was visibly distraught during his Victor's Ceremony. His mentor shielded him from the cameras and held him as he cried.
A character says before the 50th Hunger Games, that one district is the only district to not have any "living victors". Considering a victor still won the 10th Hunger Games, we know that every district had won the games at least once during its first 49 years.
Due to one district being assigned two mentors, it can be inferred that every other district had at least two victors to mentor during the second Quarter Quell.
Three districts have the largest pool of victors at the reaping for the third Quarter Quell, but every district has at least one living male victor and one living female victor.
Given the circular nature of the Victors' Village, and how unnecessarily difficult it would be to add more houses, it's likely that no district had more than twelve living victors at a time.
Career districts were already winning disproportionately by the fiftieth Hunger Games. It can be inferred that the Career alliance was formed at least a decade prior to the second Quarter Quell to allow three districts to disproportionately win.
A character recalled a male victor from "a few years ago" who only received a training score of three.
At some point in the history of the Games, one district had at least one female victor other than a known victor, as she was specified as being the only "living" female victor from her district.
Comments during the reaping recaps for the 75th Hunger Games imply that one victor wasn't the only eligible female victor from her district, and another wasn't the only eligible male victor from his district.
Since a character only sent tapes of victors other characters might have had to face in the third Quarter Quell, it was assumed that the victor of the 25th Hunger Games was deceased by then.

Films
The propaganda video shown at a district reaping in the first film appears to show four victors, two female and two male. In order of appearance, they were shown with a sword, a shield, a harpoon, and no weapon.
In an early draft of the first film script, it was mentioned during an interview that one district had produced the previous two victors.

Tribute monoliths
For a film, a series of "tribute monoliths" were meant to appear in a rescue mission scene at the Training Center. These monuments listed each district's victors, with a total of 75 across all twelve districts. However, they did not make it into the finished film. This, alongside factual and thematic inconsistencies, as well as the use of real names, makes them dubiously canon. Even before the reveal of another victor in a prequel, District 12's monolith was inconsistent with information from the books. Prior to a double victory, it was mentioned that District 12 had had "exactly two" victors: one known victor and one who was no longer alive. With the addition of two victors, the monolith should have had four names total.

Trivia
In a novel, a character said another victor was "still one of the youngest victors". Due to the ambiguity of the wording, she could have meant he was either:
A) one of the youngest people to have won the Games.
B) the youngest victor ever.
C) merely one of the youngest victors competing in the Quarter Quell.
In the film adaptation, it was mentioned that the victor "won his Games at fourteen, [the] youngest ever."
Of the thirty-one known victors, there have been 16 female (51.6%) and 15 male victors (48.4%).
One district is the only district in the trilogy known to have tributes win for two consecutive years: two sibling victors.
However, if we include the early draft of the film script, another district had two consecutive victors leading up to the 74th Hunger Games.
Prior to the 74th Hunger Games, one district only had one surviving victor, the least in all of Panem. But by the end of the war, they had the most surviving victors at three. The other four remaining victors were left as the only ones still living from each of their districts. All victors from several other districts were wiped out.
Since the victors of the 70th and 71st Hunger Games were both female and considering the early film script to be canon, the recent male victor with the training score of three most likely won the 68th or 69th, due to a character stating he won 'a few years ago'.
