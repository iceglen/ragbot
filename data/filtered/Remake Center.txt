The Remake Center was where prep teams and stylists beautified tributes for the chariot rides, which marked the opening ceremonies of the Hunger Games.

The Remake Center was at least two stories tall, with the upper floor(s) being dedicated to prep rooms and sitting rooms for the tributes. Each tribute was sat down on a table in a private room with cold white walls and floors, where they would then sit through several hours of prep.
Just off of the room where the tribute was prepped, there was a sitting room with three blank walls and a window taking up the entire fourth wall, which provided a view of the Capitol outside. Two red couches faced each other with a coffee table in between. The press of a button on this table would split the tabletop, through which a second table would rise with a spread of food for lunch.
The building's ground floor was "essentially a gigantic stable". There, each tribute boarded one of twelve horse-drawn chariots alongside their district partner.

In a film adaptation, the tributes wore hospital gowns and lay on hard metal tables similar to operating tables. Rather than each getting their own private room for the first and most invasive part of prep, they were only separated from the other tributes by curtains, like in an intensive care unit. They would be taken to a private room afterwards to await their stylist, but without any of the luxuries described in the book.

The day after the reaping for the 74th Hunger Games, a tribute met her prep team and her stylist for the first time in the Remake Center. Once all the tributes were dressed in their chariot costumes and taken to the ground floor, each district pair kept to themselves by their chariots.

Just before opening ceremonies for the 75th Hunger Games, the tributes and mentors stood around in small groups chatting in the stables, rather than staying separate like the previous year's tributes.
