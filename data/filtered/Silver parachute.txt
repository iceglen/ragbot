A parachute was used to deliver sponsor gifts to tributes during later editions of the Hunger Games.

Sponsor gifts were delivered for the first time in the 10th Hunger Games, carried out via drone. It is unknown whether Gamemakers immediately discontinued the use of drones after that year or if this was a more gradual change. It is also not known whether they tried other modes of delivery before switching over to parachutes.

Silver parachutes were in use by the 65th Hunger Games at the latest.

In the last days of a battle, refugees sought asylum in a city circle, and a group of children was positioned outside a mansion to deter an assault. On the final day of the conflict, a hovercraft dropped silver parachutes into the crowd. By this time, silver parachutes had become synonymous with aid, so children rushed to grab what they believed would be gifts. This proved fatal when the bombs attached to these parachutes detonated. Many children were injured or outright killed, and a second explosion killed the peacekeepers and medics that came to their aid. It was later revealed that this bombing was carried out by rebels.

The parachutes were made of a silver fabric that was once described as silk. In the films, the parachutes also let out beeping noises to help tributes locate them.

After a specific Hunger Games, a tribute kept a spile and a pearl wrapped up in one of the parachutes they received.
