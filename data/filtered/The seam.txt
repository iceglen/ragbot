The Seam was the most impoverished area of District 12. Coal dust covered everything in the Seam, including the streets, houses, and even the people themselves. Residents of the Seam lived in squat gray houses with a number of gates, yards, and alleys between them.

People who lived in the Seam tended to have dark straight hair, olive skin, and gray eyes. Most citizens here were coal miners; early in the morning, the streets were crowded with them as they headed to work. People generally traveled on foot since few could afford cars. Children attended school and learned about their district's trade together, preparing them for employment in the mines after their last reaping at 18 years old.

Due to rampant poverty, people often could not afford to eat, and many outright starved. However, families with children who took out tesserae were known to make flat, dense loaves or ugly drop biscuits from their year-long monthly allotments of grain. Most families in District 12 who relied on tesserae were from the Seam; the trade-off was that this increased their children's chances of being reaped for the Hunger Games. Looking old was an achievement since so many people died young, and plump people were envied because it was a sign that they weren't scraping by. Despite being the smallest, poorest district, the people knew how to dance.

Like most parts of the district, the Seam was destroyed in the bombing of District 12, and many of its people were killed. The vast majority of survivors were from the Seam. The name "Seam" comes from the actual coal seams where its citizens mined.
