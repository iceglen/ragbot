Gem of Panem was the Capitol's anthem during the Dark Days and the early days of the Hunger Games.

The song was performed in a quavery, off-key voice. In the first year of the war, a recording was played on national holidays. When district rebels surrounded the Capitol, the anthem was warbled daily through a window as an act of defiance while a home was under siege, with bombs raining down.

Gem of Panem played at the beginning of the early Hunger Games reapings. Everybody listening was required to rise and sing along.
The song was also performed during important events such as funerals. A recording of a singing performance was later replayed for a funeral. Other students fumbled for the words.
The lyrics of the song were cited when trying to make an argument against the Hunger Games, saying that the government was supposed to protect everyone and give them light, not just certain citizens. The anthem was sung for birds to mimic and harmonize.
The song was still in use 40 years later, prior to a reaping. According to an account, district citizens were supposed to sing along, but instead just mumbled whatever.
