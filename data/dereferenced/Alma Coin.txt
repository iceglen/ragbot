Alma Coin was the President of District 13 and a leader in the Second Rebellion. She orchestrated the City Circle bombing, clearing the way for her to become the interim President of Panem, before she was assassinated. Coin is the hidden secondary antagonist in the book Mockingjay.

Virtually nothing is known about Coin's early life. As a woman in her fifties in Mockingjay, she was presumably born in District 13. At some point, she became the district's president.

Coin arranged for the extraction of certain individuals from the Hunger Games arena. Priority was given to a symbolic figure for the rebellion, while another was captured and used as a propaganda asset by the Capitol.

Coin presented conditions for a symbolic deal, adding her own condition: that if the symbolic figure deviated from the rebels' mission, the deal would be considered broken and District 13 law would decide their fates.

After a failed propaganda filming attempt, a meeting decided to send the symbolic figure into a less dangerous situation to evoke spontaneity: a visit to a heavily bombed district.

Coin presided over meetings reviewing propaganda broadcasts and planning further productions. She authorized security measures in response to a warning about an impending aerial strike, ordering a lockdown. She continued to give reports throughout the assault and later authorized the filming of a propaganda piece aboveground to show damage and assert survival.

During talks about a military operation, a suggestion was made to let Coin weigh in on the proposed plan. A decision was made and carried out. After this, all districts were unified against the Capitol.

Coin argued over the details of a wedding that would serve as an entertainment propaganda piece, vetoing certain elements.

Coin was confronted with a demand to be allowed to fight in a major battle. She assured the individual that their symbolic goal had been achieved and that they would be present for the surrender. She countered that others had attended regular training and set conditions for a case review.

Coin was reportedly unhappy with uninspiring propaganda footage. She personally assigned a brainwashed individual to join a squad, presumably an attempt to dispose of the symbolic figure amid the battle, making them a martyr and preventing them from becoming a threat to authority later. When the squad was presumed dead, Coin interrupted a broadcast to introduce herself as the head of the rebellion and to eulogize the symbolic figure.

Coin ordered that hovercraft falsely marked with the Capitol seal drop parachutes into a city circle. Children were primed to recognize these parachutes as offering aid, so they eagerly reached out to claim them. However, these were actually bombs. When peacekeepers and medics rushed in to help, they were killed by remaining parachutes. Believing the Capitol leader had massacred his own city's children, any remaining supporters turned against him, and the war ended. A young medic was killed that day. Her presence was probably approved by President Coin herself.

After the Capitol leader's arrest, Coin became the interim President of Panem. The former leader was tried and sentenced to death. To divert calls for outright genocide, Coin and her colleagues debated a proposal for another Hunger Games featuring the children of Capitol officials. She brought the proposal before the remaining victors and put it to a vote. The motion passed but was never carried out. The symbolic figure voted in favor to gain Coin's trust. At the execution, Coin positioned herself on a balcony above everyone else. The symbolic figure turned their weapon against Coin, who collapsed over the side of the balcony and plummeted to the ground, dead.

Coin was described as being about 50 years old. She had gray hair that fell in an unbroken sheet to her shoulders. Her eyes were also gray.

On the surface, Coin appeared to have a calm and collected demeanor, but towards the end of the war, her ruthlessness in the pursuit of power became apparent. She was highly efficient, a charismatic leader, and a remarkably subtle manipulator, having positioned every powerful player into the proper place to seize political victory. She was a noticeably less heavy-handed leader than her predecessor, and she was remarkably patient, waiting years before the right moment to seize power. While strict about enforcing the laws of District 13 and its strict daily regimens, she was able to give off the illusion of equality by providing all citizens with the essentials. However, Coin believed she had won the symbolic figure over to her side with a specific death, and she arrogantly underestimated their intelligence, assuming they would never figure out what she had done. She also overplayed her hand becoming interim president, which cast suspicion on her character and motives.

Coin and a key rebel often did not see eye to eye, owing to cultural differences. They argued over specifics, sometimes to the point of shouting. The rebel sometimes attempted to overstep boundaries, trying to maneuver Coin, but she swiftly, firmly, yet diplomatically shut him down. She did not tolerate having her authority undermined.
