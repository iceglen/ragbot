Shamus was the goat that belonged to the Covey. When visitors first came to the Covey's home, Shamus was in the Meadow. After an interaction, Shamus tugged on a visitor's collar, as she needed milking. She was then hitched to a post to keep her in place. Another member took a visitor out to milk Shamus instead. Shamus was said to be able to give a gallon of milk a day at her best, and the members of the Covey were attempting to skim enough cream for butter. A butter churn was created, but the Covey weren't sure if it would work, since goat's milk didn't separate the same way cow milk did. A visitor also stated that Shamus's milk reminded him of his childhood. Shamus is noted as being a boy's name. It's an alternate spelling of another name, which is a variation of another name. Shamus is one of two named goats in the books, both female. Her name has the same root as another character's name.
