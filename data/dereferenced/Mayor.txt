A mayor was a citizen, presumably appointed by the Capitol, who ran their district's local government. This position afforded them a degree of wealth and privilege unavailable to most district citizens.

The mayor's role in local government amounted to carrying out the will of the Capitol within their district. They had no apparent hand in policymaking, but they could put pressure on the Peacekeepers to enforce laws more harshly. The mayor's other known duties were largely ceremonial.

Each district's mayor was obligated to host the annual reaping for the Hunger Games. First, the mayor gave a scripted speech about the disasters and wars that led to the creation of Panem, leading into a recollection of the Dark Days. Then they read a list of the district's past victors before introducing the escort, who would conduct the actual reaping. Once the tributes were chosen and called onto the stage, the mayor concluded the event by reading aloud the Treaty of Treason and having the tributes shake hands.
During the first ten years of the Games, the escort role did not yet exist, so mayors selected and read out the tributes' names themselves. That made it simple for a mayor to rig the results.

Mayors and their families enjoyed a number of privileges. For example, they had access to classified information not available to the rest of the district population.
Mayors were also considerably wealthy compared to other district citizens. They could feed their families and still have money to spare, so although their children were not exempt from the reaping, they would never have to sign up for tesserae, so it was less likely that their names would be picked.
Despite their advantages, mayors were subject to the same travel rules as ordinary district citizens. They were not allowed to go to the Capitol unless invited.
