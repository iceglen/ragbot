Tracker jackers are genetically engineered wasp muttations. They are genetically programmed to attack anyone or anything that disturbs their nest. They follow their targets far away from their nests, unlike natural vespids.

Tracker jackers are larger than normal wasps, and they have solid gold bodies. They have stingers that transfer venom to their victims.

Tracker jacker envenomation is extremely painful and causes localized swelling that progresses dramatically in minutes. Without treatment, intense pain can persist for days. The venom can be lethal in a relatively low dose; multiple stings in quick succession usually lead to swift death.

Tracker jacker venom is engineered to target the part of the brain that generates fear, creating terrifying hallucinations that can drive a person to madness. The venom causes visual, auditory, olfactory, and tactile hallucinations. It can also be used to "hijack" memories in a form of torture, where a memory becomes subconsciously associated with fear and pain and is warped.

Like real vespids, smoke can be used to sedate tracker jackers and minimize the risk of being stung. A certain type of leaf can draw out the poison from stings. These leaves can be applied by chewing and applying them topically.
