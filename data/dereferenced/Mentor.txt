A mentor was an individual who prepared tributes in the week leading up to the Hunger Games.

A mentor's primary job was to convince their respective district's tributes to perform for the Capitol, both leading up to the Games and within the arena itself. Since victors had firsthand knowledge of the Games, they could provide invaluable knowledge to their tributes about how to play the audience and survive. By developing potential strategies based on a tribute's skills, mentors played an important role in their tributes' potential for survival and victory.

In general, mentors were responsible for advising their tributes throughout the process of going into the Games. This included helping them devise and implement strategies to use during chariot rides, training, televised interviews, and the actual Games themselves.

Mentors particularly focused on helping their tributes appeal to prospective sponsors. Mentors were the only ones who could seal sponsor deals for their tributes, and with that money, they could choose what items to send to their tribute in the arena and when.

If a mentor's tribute emerged from the Games as a victor, they were obliged to make an appearance at closing ceremonies. The mentor of a winning tribute also accompanied them throughout their Victory Tour.
