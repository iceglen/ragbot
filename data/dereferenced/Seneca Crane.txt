Seneca Crane was the Head Gamemaker of the 72nd, 73rd, and 74th Hunger Games, in charge of coordinating game play and ordering the creation of obstacles into the arena. At the end of the 74th Games, he allowed both tributes to emerge as victors, rather than allow them to commit suicide and thus have no victor at all. This decision did not sit well with the President, and Crane was dead by the time of the Victory Tour six months later.

As the Head Gamemaker, he was a bloodthirsty, cruel individual. He openly showed pleasure in creating various dangers in the arena for the tributes, such as mutts, as well as watching the tributes kill one another. He seemed to like his job more for the fun and drama of it, not for the murder. However, he was not completely corrupted and showed a degree of mercy by allowing both tributes to survive. He was uncomfortable when interacting with the President, and he did not fully agree with the harsh regime. It was not until the double suicide threat that he finally decided to not go along with everything. This ultimately led to his execution.

As a result of his actions, he inadvertently paved the way for a rebellion. Had he killed both tributes, the idea of rebellion would have died along with them. While his granting mercy was likely an effort to save face more than anything, it revealed an unexpected chink in the armor; the districts now realized that despite its power, the Capitol could be made to look foolish, and its agenda undermined. More to the point, his actions ruined one of the primary purposes of the Hunger Games - they were supposed to be a reminder that the Capitol controlled their very existence. His actions would later be repeated by a subsequent Head Gamemaker, who effectively collapsed the tradition of the Hunger Games when he secretly devised a plan to rescue a tribute from the Arena.
