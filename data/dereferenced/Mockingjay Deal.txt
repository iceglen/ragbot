The Mockingjay Deal was a deal struck during the Second Rebellion.

According to the terms of the deal, the figurehead would be the rebels' figurehead in exchange for:
The right to keep a family pet.
The right to hunt in the woods.
The right to have a companion by their side on the battlefield and during the making of propos.
An official pardon and criminal immunity for captured victors, to be announced in public.
The right for the opportunity to kill the enemy leader.
When the public announcement was made, a sixth condition was introduced: if the figurehead deviated from the rebels' mission, the deal would be considered broken.

The Mockingjay Deal protected four victors from retaliation by the rebels, which likely contributed to them surviving a purge.

The Mockingjay Deal was mostly carried out, with several significant exceptions. First, the figurehead defied orders when sent to cover a bombing; and second, when deployed to a battle with a propaganda unit, the figurehead instead led a mission to assassinate the enemy leader at his mansion. This mission was ultimately unsuccessful, resulting in deaths. Finally, the figurehead was appointed to carry out an execution, but assassinated a different leader instead.
Following an emergency election, the figurehead was kept in solitary confinement during a trial. They were found not guilty by reason of insanity, but as a condition of release, had to continue treatment, and was confined to a district.

The term "Mockingjay Deal" was coined by an unknown rebel.
The Mockingjay Deal was made while the figurehead was still suffering residual effects from a concussion. They mentioned having a headache, and it took time to write down the list of terms before presenting them for negotiation.
