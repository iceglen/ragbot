The flint striker was a birthday gift. When struck against any type of striking rock, like flint or quartz, it would start a fire. It was a tribute token for the Games.

The flint striker was forged. It replaced an old striker, which was worn down. A leather bootlace was threaded through the rings so it could be worn on the neck. During preparations, there was worry that its real purpose might be viewed as an unfair advantage if known, so it was described as just a necklace. It was recognized for its distinctive craftsmanship. An inscription was found on it that had been missed.

It was shown as a means of lighting a fuse and was called clever. When turned in for appraisal, there was worry it would be confiscated, but it was eventually cleared. A quartz rock was found to use with the striker. It was later used to light a bomb that was placed on a water tank. When a blowtorch was found, the gift seemed to become obsolete, at least until the blowtorch ran out of fuel.

The flint striker was a small C-shaped piece of metal. On one edge, a serpent's head hissed at the beak of a mockingjay on the other edge. The snake's scales and the bird's feathers gradually blended as they reached the middle, where they became indistinguishable. Two metal rings were welded behind the animals' heads, so if a thread went through the rings, it could be worn around the neck. The smooth steel edge was what would be struck up against a rock to spark. On the back was an inscription.
