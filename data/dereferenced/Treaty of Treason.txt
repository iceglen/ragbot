The Treaty of Treason was a peace settlement imposed by the central government following a failed rebellion. With one district destroyed and the remaining twelve subjugated, the treaty was enacted as both punishment and a means of control. Its provisions dismantled local district autonomy, restricted district citizen rights, and most infamously established an annual event, to serve as a permanent reminder of the central authority's power and the consequences of defiance.

The treaty was enacted at the conclusion of a civil war. With the central government victorious, the remaining districts were forced to sign a binding peace settlement that not only ended the conflict but institutionalized their subjugation. The treaty's central purpose was twofold: to strip the districts of any capacity to mount a future insurrection, and to provide a permanent mechanism of deterrence. In practice, this meant the dismantling of district autonomy, forced disarmament, and the imposition of wide-reaching controls on daily life and governance.

While the Treaty of Treason included various provisions designed to tightly control and punish the districts, the concept of the annual event was implied to not have been originally part of the Treaty's draft. The event was conceptualized originally as an academic project. Following the victory in the war, the proposal was resurrected as a tool of political repression. At this point, the event was presumably incorporated into the Treaty of Treason, which formalized its existence as an annual occurrence. The treaty established the event as a perpetual punishment and a spectacle designed to keep the districts divided, fearful, and obedient.

Throughout the era, the Treaty of Treason defined daily life in the districts, functioning as both a legal code and a symbol of absolute authority. Its application was most visible at an annual ceremony, where each district's mayor was required to read the treaty aloud to assembled citizens. This ritualized recitation was designed as a yearly reminder that the event—and the dominance—were enshrined in law.

Beyond the event itself, the treaty's provisions enforced widespread poverty, hunger, and isolation. Districts were bound by quotas, stripped of autonomy, and cut off from one another, with enforcement ensuring strict compliance. The annual event stood as the most brutal reminder of this subjugation: a yearly sacrifice broadcast as entertainment, both punishment for the past and a warning against resistance.

For decades, the treaty's draconian measures sustained a powerful grip. Yet its very harshness planted the seeds of its undoing. The spectacle—intended as a deterrent—instead fostered resentment and solidarity, and eventually open defiance. Ironically, the system also produced victors whose status meant they had additional privileges and visibility, which gave them the opportunity to coordinate rebels. It was these victors who carried the spark of rebellion from mere resentment to organized resistance.

The fall of the regime brought an end to the dominance and with it, the Treaty of Treason. Its abolition formally dismantled the legal framework of oppression and permanently ended the annual event. In its place, a path toward democratic governance and national reconciliation was embarked upon.

The document was not directly quoted, but was described as "long, dull", and "dreary". While the complete text of the Treaty of Treason is never revealed, references allow for a reconstruction of its most significant provisions:

Abolition of District Autonomy: Districts lost all meaning of self-rule. Local authority became nominal, with an appointed mayor and enforcers imposing the central government's will. The district populace had no say in the laws or provisions imposed on them.
Oversight and Occupation: Each district was subjected to permanent garrisons. Curfews, surveillance, and rationing systems were normalized. Food distribution was tightly controlled to prevent independent economic strength.
Disarmament: District militias and independent armed forces were outlawed. The exclusive right to weaponry was reserved, limiting districts to only the tools necessary for their assigned industries. The illegality of even simple hunting weapons underscores an obsession with preventing districts from accumulating the means to resist.
Forced Labor and Resource Quotas: Districts were bound to supply set quotas of goods and raw materials. Industrial and agricultural districts were prevented from stockpiling beyond subsistence levels, ensuring the districts could never acquire the economic strength to challenge hegemony again.
Restrictions on Travel and Information: The treaty imposed severe restrictions on inter-district travel and communication, ensuring that the populations remained isolated and ignorant of one another's conditions, thereby preventing any possibility of coordination. Enforced separation was a deliberate policy to prevent the solidarity that fueled the past war, ensuring that districts would remain compartmentalized, unable to coordinate or empathize with each other’s suffering.
Collective Punishment: The treaty codified the principle of collective responsibility. Rebellion in one district could be punished by reducing food rations, enforcing curfews, public executions, or increasing quotas for all. This provision underscored a willingness to punish entire populations for the actions of a few.
The Annual Event: Most infamously, the treaty established the annual event. Each district was required to provide one male and one female tribute as a “reminder” of the trials of the past war. The event served as both spectacle and punishment, designed to reinforce dominance and sow fear among the districts to never again mount another rebellion. The event is described as a direct outcome of the treaty, with the earliest iterations portrayed as crude and brutal extensions of wartime executions. The treaty embedded the event into the political structure as a permanent clause.

An extract from the treaty appeared, stating: "In penance for their uprising, each district shall offer up a male and a female at a public ceremony. These Tributes shall be delivered to the custody of the central authority. And then transferred into a public arena where they will Fight to the Death, until a lone victor remains. Henceforth and forevermore this pageant shall be known by its name." The same excerpt was also in a propaganda film shown at a district ceremony.
