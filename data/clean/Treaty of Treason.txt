
"The Treaty of Treason gave us the new laws to guarantee peace and, as our yearly reminder that the Dark Days must never be repeated, it gave us the Hunger Games."
—Katniss Everdeen's description[src]
The Treaty of Treason was a peace settlement imposed by the Capitol following the Dark Days, the failed rebellion in which thirteen of Panem's districts rose up against the central government. With District 13 destroyed and the remaining twelve districts subjugated, the Capitol enacted the treaty as both punishment and a means of control. Its provisions dismantled local district autonomy, restricted district citizen rights, and most infamously established the annual Hunger Games, to serve as a permanent reminder of the Capitol's authority and the consequences of defiance.[1]

History[]
Background[]
The Treaty of Treason was enacted at the conclusion of the Dark Days, the civil war in which the districts of Panem rebelled against the Capitol. Although initially united in their resistance, the rebellion collapsed following the destruction of District 13, which had spearheaded military production materiel. With the Capitol victorious, the remaining twelve districts were forced to sign a binding peace settlement that not only ended the conflict but institutionalized their subjugation.
The treaty's central purpose was twofold: to strip the districts of any capacity to mount a future insurrection, and to provide the Capitol with a permanent mechanism of deterrence. In practice, this meant the dismantling of district autonomy, forced disarmament, and the imposition of wide-reaching controls on daily life and governance.

Creation of the Hunger Games[]

"The assignment was to create a punishment for one’s enemies so extreme that they would never be allowed to forget how they had wronged you."
—Casca Highbottom[src]
While the Treaty of Treason included various provisions designed to tightly control and punish the districts, the concept of the Hunger Games are implied to not have been originally part of the Treaty's draft. The Games were conceptualized originally as an academic project of Casca Highbottom[2] while a student at the University under the tutelage of Dr. Volumnia Gaul. As a final assignment, Gaul tasked her class to produce a punishment so extreme that one's enemies would never forget how they wronged them. Highbottom partnered with his best friend, Crassus Snow, who deliberately intoxicated him during their brainstorming process. In a drunken haze, Highbottom drafted the basic outline that would become the Hunger Games. Once Highbottom sobered up, he was horrified that Snow had turned the proposal in, ensuring Gaul had it on record.[3]
Following the Capitol's victory in the Dark Days, Dr. Gaul resurrected Highbottom's proposal as a tool of political repression. She presented him publicly to the country as "the architect of the Hunger Games."[3] At this point, the Games were presumably incorporated into the Treaty of Treason, which formalized their existence as an annual event. The treaty established the Games as a perpetual punishment and a spectacle designed to keep the districts divided, fearful, and obedient. In addition to enshrining the Hunger Games, the Treaty also introduced new legislation,[1] though the specific contents of these laws remains unclear.

Hunger Games period[]
Throughout the Hunger Games era, the Treaty of Treason defined daily life in the districts, functioning as both a legal code and a symbol of the Capitol's absolute authority. Its application was most visible at the annual reaping, where each district's mayor was required to read the treaty aloud to assembled citizens before the tributes were selected.[4] This ritualized recitation, performed under the eye of Peacekeepers, was designed as a yearly reminder that the Games—and the Capitol's dominance—were enshrined in law.
Beyond the Games themselves, the treaty's provisions enforced widespread poverty, hunger, and isolation. Districts were bound by quotas, stripped of autonomy, and cut off from one another, with Peacekeepers ensuring strict compliance. The Hunger Games stood as the most brutal reminder of this subjugation: the annual sacrifice of children broadcast as entertainment, both punishment for the Dark Days and a warning against resistance.
For seventy-five years, the treaty's draconian measures sustained the Capitol's grip. Yet its very harshness planted the seeds of its undoing. The spectacle of the Hunger Games—intended as a deterrent—instead fostered resentment, solidarity, and eventually open defiance that built up over decades. Ironically, the system also produced victors whose celebrity status meant they had additional privileges and visibility, as well as the occasional ability to move between the districts and the Capitol, all of which combined gave them the opportunity to coordinate rebels across Panem. It was these victors who carried the spark of rebellion from mere resentment to organized resistance. In the end, the treaty that was meant to prevent another uprising became one of the central forces ensuring the inevitability of the Second Rebellion and the eventual downfall of the Capitol's hegemony.

Post-Hunger Games period[]
The fall of the Snow regime during the Second Rebellion brought an end to the Capitol's dominance and with it, the Treaty of Treason.[5] Its abolition formally dismantled the legal framework of oppression and permanently ended the Hunger Games. In its place, Panem embarked on a fragile, but hopeful path toward democratic governance and national reconciliation under the auspice of President Paylor. For the first time, the districts were no longer subjects of the Capitol, but equal citizens of a united republic.[6]

Contents[]
Novels[]
The document was not directly quoted in any of the books, but Katniss Everdeen described it as "long, dull", and "dreary".[1] While the complete text of the Treaty of Treason is never revealed, the combined references in The Hunger Games series allow for a reconstruction of its most significant provisions:

Abolition of District Autonomy: Districts lost all meaning of self-rule. Local authority became nominal, with a Capitol appointed mayor and Peacekeepers enforcing the central government's will and authority. As such, the district populace had no say in the laws or provisions imposed on them.
Capitol Oversight and Occupation: Each district was subjected to permanent garrisons of Peacekeepers. Curfews, surveillance, and rationing systems were normalized, with varying levels of enforcement depending on the district. Food distribution was tightly controlled to prevent independent economic strength. Any hint of rebellion was met with draconian enforcement of law and order.
Disarmament: District militias and independent armed forces were outlawed. The Capitol reserved the exclusive right to weaponry, limiting districts to only the tools necessary for their assigned industries. In Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, Sejanus Plinth reflects on how the treaty ensured districts could not maintain organized arsenals beyond what the Capitol sanctioned. Generations later, this disarmament filtered down to ordinary life: Katniss noted that possession of her bow and arrows was technically illegal, but tolerated in District 12 because the game she hunted supplemented meager rations, even for the mayor and Peacekeepers, who she and Gale traded with. The illegality of even simple hunting weapons underscores the Capitol’s obsession with preventing districts from accumulating the means to resist.
Forced Labor and Resource Quotas: Districts were bound to supply the Capitol with set quotas of goods and raw materials. Industrial and agricultural districts were prevented from stockpiling beyond subsistence levels, ensuring the districts could never acquire the economic strength to challenge the Capitol's hegemony again.
Restrictions on Travel and Information: The treaty imposed severe restrictions on inter-district travel and communication, ensuring that the populations remained isolated and ignorant of one another's conditions, thereby preventing any possibility of coordination against the Capitol.
In The Hunger Games, Katniss repeatedly reflects on her ignorance of life in other districts until the Hunger Games themselves reveal glimpses of them. The Tribute Parade in the Capitol is explicitly described as the first and only time most citizens see children from other districts and learn what they are like. Katniss remarks that her schooling only ever touches on the other's districts basic industries and nothing more,
In Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, the early Games are described as one of the few occasions where districts are brought together under Capitol supervision, underscoring that enforced separation was a deliberate policy.
In Sunrise on the Reaping, Snow frames this provision as essential to prevent the solidarity that fueled the Dark Days, ensuring that districts would remain compartmentalized, unable to coordinate or empathize with each other’s suffering.
Collective Punishment: The treaty codified the principle of collective responsibility. Rebellion in one district could be punished by reducing food rations, enforcing curfews, public executions, or increasing quotas for all. This provision underscored the Capitol’s willingness to punish entire populations for the actions of a few.
The Hunger Games: Most infamously, the treaty established the annual Hunger Games. Each district was required to provide one male and one female tribute between the ages of 12 and 18 as a “reminder” of the trials and tribulations of the Dark Days. The Games served as both spectacle and punishment, designed to reinforce Capitol dominance and sow fear among the districts to never again mount another rebellion.
In Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, the Games are described as a direct outcome of the treaty, with the earliest iterations portrayed as crude and brutal extensions of wartime executions.
In Sunrise on the Reaping, Coriolanus Snow reflects on how the treaty embedded the Games into the Capitol’s political structure as a permanent clause.
Film adaptation[]
An extract from the treaty appeared at the beginning of The Hunger Games film:


"In penance for their uprising, each district shall offer up a male and a female between the ages of 12 and 18 at a public "Reaping." These Tributes shall be delivered to the custody of the Capitol. And then transferred into a public arena where they will Fight to the Death, until a lone victor remains. Henceforth and forevermore this pageant shall be known as the Hunger Games."
—Opening quotation from "the Treaty of the Treason"[src]
