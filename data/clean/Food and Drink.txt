Food is a major theme in The Hunger Games trilogy, as well as in the prequel novel The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes. This theme manifests in a variety of ways: descriptions of meals, whether simple or elaborate, are a regular feature of the narratives; for several years, putting food on her family's table was the primary focus of Katniss Everdeen’s existence; and it was through food (specifically bread) that Katniss Everdeen and Peeta Mellark made the connection that set many later events of the trilogy in motion.

Symbolism[]
On a more symbolic level, descriptions of food often help to set the tone for a particular scene: They help to distinguish between different areas of Panem, and reveal details about society and culture. In poorer areas, such as the Seam in District 12, the people have a very hardscrabble existence, and the foods listed tend to be very basic and/or low in quality, such as bread made from ration grain and dishes made from whatever ingredients can be found in the surrounding woods. In contrast, cuisine in the Capitol is often described as very over-the-top, made with an endless variety of expensive and high quality ingredients, and served in large portions or buffets. Even within the Capitol, however, not everyone sets the same table, and certain characters struggle with basic food needs as much as anyone in the districts. Aside from such distinctions, different types of food are associated with specific areas of Panem, depending of the district's industry, climate, and economic/demographic makeup.
In the Training Center, the tributes' rooms are equipped with machines that deliver freshly prepared food to the rooms' occupants in less than a minute.[1]
