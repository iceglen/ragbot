"Gem of Panem" was the Capitol's anthem during the Dark Days and the early days of the Hunger Games.[1]

History[]
The song was performed by the Grandma'am, Coriolanus Snow's grandmother, in a quavery, off-key voice. In the first year of the war, she played the recording on national holidays for young Coriolanus and Tigris. When district rebels surrounded the Capitol, the Grandma'am began to warble the anthem through a window daily as an act of defiance while their home was under siege, bombs raining down around her and her family.[1]
"Gem of Panem" played at the beginning of the early Hunger Games reapings. Everybody listening was required to rise and sing along.[2]
The song was also performed during important events such as funerals. Due to his knowledge of the song from his grandmother, Coriolanus was able to give a strong performance of it during Arachne Crane's funeral, impressing his instructors and President Ravinstill.[3] A recording of his singing was later replayed for the funeral of Diana and Apollo Ring.[4] Despite Coriolanus' familiarity with the anthem, other Academy students fumbled for the words.[2][5]
Sejanus Plinth cited the lyrics of the song when trying to make an argument against the Hunger Games, saying that Panem's government was supposed to protect everyone and give them light, not just citizens of the Capitol.[6] In District 12, when Dr. Kay first introduced Coriolanus to the mockingjays, he sung the anthem for the birds to mimic and harmonize.[7]
The song was still in use 40 years later, prior to the reaping for the 50th Hunger Games. According to Haymitch Abernathy, district citizens were supposed to sing along, but instead just mumbled whatever.[8]
