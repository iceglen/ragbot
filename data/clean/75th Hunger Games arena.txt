The 75th Hunger Games arena is where the 75th Hunger Games or third Quarter Quell took place. This small arena was divided into twelve sections, with a Gamemaker trap in each wedge being activated twice a day at a particular hour.[1] Following the abolition of the Hunger Games, this and all other arenas were destroyed and replaced with memorials.[2]

Terrain[]
 	 	 	 		 			 		 		 		 			Concept art of the Cornucopia for The Hunger Games: Catching Fire. 		 	 
The tributes were launched on their pedestals in a ring around the Cornucopia, which sat on an island in the center of a round salt lake. The sky above them was pink. Rather than offering food and other supplies like usual, the Cornucopia only held weapons this year.[3] Its tail originally pointed to the twelve o'clock sector, but the island could be spun around to disorient tributes, as it was on the second day.[4]
Like a wheel, spokes divided the salt lake into twelve sections, with two tributes in each. These spokes ran from the Cornucopia to the beach. Beyond the beach was a dense jungle, invisibly divided into twelve sections as well. A high-voltage force field enclosed the entire arena in a dome.[3]

Mechanics[]
The arena was set up like a clock, with each wedge holding a different threat activated by the hour. The cycle began at midnight on the first night when a bell tolled twelve times, marking the start of the second day.[5] Of the arena's twelve sections, only six are known concretely, with tributes speculating on two others.

The Zones[]
Hour 12-1: Lightning bolts struck a specific tree,[5] though there were identical trees in each sector to confuse the tributes.[4] Those in the rebel alliance wrapped a coil of wire around this tree and, securing the wire to one of her arrows, Katniss Everdeen shot at the force field and blew it out.[6] This allowed rebels to extract her, Finnick Odair, and Beetee Latier from the arena to District 13.[7]
Hour 1-2: Seeing last hour's storm, dehydrated tributes eagerly awaited regular rainfall, only to be soaked and blinded with blood rain.[1]

Food and water sources[]
 	 	 	 		 			 		 		 		 			Katniss drinking water from a tree using the spile. 		 	 Unlike most years, food and drink were not available at the Cornucopia, and there were no tools to facilitate desalinating water from the salt lake.[3] However, the trees were full of fresh water; one could drill a hole into the bark and place a spile to extract the water inside.[5]
In terms of food, tributes could forage for edible nuts, or they could hunt some of the jungle's ordinary, if unfamiliar, wildlife. Peeta Mellark discovered that meat could be tossed into the force field to be cooked.[5] Those with sharp weapons like Finnick's trident could spear fish in the salt lake, or they could bring in a shellfish if they fashioned a net; these things could be eaten raw. Fair swimmers could even dive for oysters.[11] Sponsors could also send gifts of food to tributes by parachute like usual.[1]

Trivia[]
For The Hunger Games: Catching Fire, the Cornucopia scenes were filmed at The Beach at Clayton County International Park in Jonesboro, Georgia. Beach scenes were filmed at Kawela Beach in Kawela Bay, Oʻahu, Hawaiʻi.
Jungle scenes were filmed in Waimea Valley and Manoa, also both in Oʻahu.
Only five tributes were killed by the arena's timed traps.
In the 50th Hunger Games or second Quarter Quell, the only sources of safe food and water in the arena were the Cornucopia and rainwater. In the third Quarter Quell, this was reversed: the Cornucopia only provided weapons, so food and water had to be acquired from the arena itself or through sponsors.
