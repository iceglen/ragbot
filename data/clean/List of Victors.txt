Known victors[]
There have been 75 victors throughout the history of the Hunger Games: one for each year from the 1st through 73rd, and then an unprecedented double victory in the 74th Hunger Games.[1] No victor was crowned for the 75th Hunger Games.[2] All victors were between the ages of 14 and 18 at the time of their victory.[3]
At the time of the reaping for the 75th Hunger Games, 59 victors were still alive, and Lucy Gray Baird's fate is unknown, so fifteen died prior to the third Quarter Quell. The Career districts— District 1, District 2, and District 4— had the most victors, but each district had at least one surviving male and female.[4] Eighteen more died in the Quell,[2] and by the end of the Second Rebellion, only seven victors were shown alive. This means that another 34 presumably died during the Victors' Purge and the rebellion.[5]
Only 27 victors were mentioned in The Hunger Games trilogy. Two more (Porter Millicent Tripp and Augustus Braun) were revealed by the promotional website Capitol Couture, and another appeared in The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, for a total of 30 known victors. Unknown victors[]
Since there have been 75 victors in the history of the Hunger Games and 31 are known, there are still 44 unknown victors.

Books[]
From the 1st-9th Hunger Games, Districts 1 and 2 already produced more victors than other districts. 4 and 11 were also considered contenders,[19] so they may have turned out victors as well, or at least had tributes place highly.
Katniss states that District 12 does the worst in the games, with just two victors. By this, it can be assumed that every district from District 1 to District 11 has at least three victors by the seventy-fourth Hunger Games.
When Haymitch was "little", District 4 had a male victor who was visibly distraught during his Victor's Ceremony. His mentor, Mags, shielded him from the cameras and held him as he cried.[20]
Haymitch says before the 50th Hunger Games, that District 12 is the only district to not have any "living victors". Considering Lucy Gray Baird still won the 10th Hunger Games, we know that every district had won the games at least once during its first 49 years.[20]
Due to District 12 being assigned two mentors, it can be inferred that every other district had at least two victors to mentor during the second Quarter Quell.
Districts 1, 2, and 4 have the largest pool of victors at the reaping for the third Quarter Quell, but every district has at least one living male victor and one living female victor.
Given the circular nature of the Victors' Village, and how unnecessarily difficult it would be to add more houses, it's likely that no district had more than twelve living victors at a time.
Career districts were already winning disproportionately by the fiftieth Hunger Games. It can be inferred that the Career alliance was formed at least a decade prior to the second Quarter Quell to allow three districts to disproportionately win.
Katniss recalled a male victor from "a few years ago" who only received a training score of three.[21]
At some point in the history of the Games, District 7 had at least one female victor other than Johanna, as she was specified as being the only "living" female victor from her district.[4]
Effie Trinket's comments during the reaping recaps for the 75th Hunger Games imply that Cecelia wasn't the only eligible female victor from District 8, and Chaff wasn't the only eligible male victor from District 11.[4]
Since Effie only sent Katniss and Peeta tapes of victors they might have had to face in the third Quarter Quell, Peeta assumed that the victor of the 25th Hunger Games was deceased by then.[4]
Films[]
The propaganda video shown at the District 12 reaping in the first film appears to show four victors, two female and two male. In order of appearance, they were shown with a sword, a shield, a harpoon, and no weapon.[22]
In an early draft of the first film script, Caesar Flickerman mentioned to Cato during his interview that District 2 had produced the previous two victors (of the 72nd and 73rd Hunger Games, respectively).[23]

Tribute monoliths[]
For Mockingjay - Part 1, a series of "tribute monoliths" were meant to appear in the rescue mission scene at the Training Center. These monuments listed each district's victors,[29] with a total of 75 across all twelve districts. However, they did not make it into the finished film. This, alongside factual and thematic inconsistencies, as well as the use of real actors' names, makes them dubiously canon. Furthermore, many of the names below have been transcribed by fans from low-quality images, so they should be taken with a grain of salt. Known victors are bolded.

Trivia[]
In the novel Catching Fire, Katniss said Finnick was "still one of the youngest victors".[3] Due to the ambiguity of the wording, she could have meant he was either:
A) one of the youngest people to have won the Games.
B) the youngest victor ever.
C) merely one of the youngest victors competing in the Quarter Quell.
In the film adaptation, Haymitch mentioned that Finnick "won his Games at fourteen, [the] youngest ever."[7]
Of the thirty-one known victors, there have been 16 female (51.6%) and 15 male victors (48.4%).
District 1 is the only district in the trilogy known to have tributes win for two consecutive years: Gloss and Cashmere. They are also the only known sibling victors.[4]
However, if we include the early draft of the film script, District 2 had two consecutive victors leading up to the 74th Hunger Games.[23]
Prior to the 74th Hunger Games, District 12 only had one surviving victor, the least in all of Panem.[32] But by the end of the war, they had the most surviving victors at three (Katniss, Peeta, and Haymitch). The other four remaining victors (Enobaria, Beetee, Annie, and Johanna) were left as the only ones still living from each of their districts (2, 3, 4, and 7, respectively). All victors from 1, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, and 11 were wiped out.[5]
Since the victors of the 70th and 71st Hunger Games were both female and considering the early film script to be canon, the recent male victor with the training score of three[21] most likely won the 68th or 69th, due to Katniss stating he won 'a few years ago'.
