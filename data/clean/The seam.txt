Description[]
Coal dust covered everything in the Seam, including the streets, houses, and even the people themselves. Residents of the Seam lived in "squat gray houses"[1] with a number of gates, yards, and alleys between them.[2]

Katniss Everdeen's house[]
Main article: Katniss Everdeen's house
Katniss Everdeen and her family lived in a "squat little place"[2] near the edge of the Seam, a few gates down from the Meadow.[1]

Other residences[]
The Covey's house was located at the edge of the Seam, close to the Meadow and the woods.[3]
Prior to his house's burning, Haymitch Abernathy, his mother Willamae, and his brother Sid lived in the Seam.[4]
Hazelle Hawthorne and her four children lived a few minutes away from the Everdeens on foot, when Katniss cut through alleyways and backyards.[2]
Leevy and her younger brother lived a few houses down from the Everdeens.[5]
History[]
Hunger Games[]
Lucy Gray Baird and Jessup Diggs were reaped to participate in the 10th Hunger Games.[6] Lucy Gray went on to become District 12's first victor.[7]
Forty years later, Louella McCoy and Wyatt Callow were both reaped from the Seam for the 50th Hunger Games, as was Haymitch Abernathy, the victor that year.[8] The male and female tribute in the 73rd Hunger Games were both from the Seam,[9] and the same was true of Primrose Everdeen, who was reaped for the 74th Hunger Games the next year.[1] However, her older sister Katniss volunteered to go in her place.[10]
Following Katniss's joint victory with Peeta Mellark, hungry children from the Seam benefited from monthly Parcel Day food deliveries. These were given to the district by the Capitol as part of the victors' winnings.[11] However, after only a few months, Parcel Day deliveries and regular shipments of tessera grain and oil started to arrive spoiled and "defiled by rodents".[12]
Katniss and Haymitch were both reaped again for the 75th Hunger Games, but Peeta volunteered to compete in place of Haymitch.[13]

Second Rebellion[]
Like most parts of the district, the Seam was destroyed in the bombing of District 12, and many of its people were killed. However, fewer than a dozen merchants escaped, so the vast majority of survivors were from the Seam. Gale Hawthorne led refugees to the lake in the woods outside District 12, and upon discovery and rescue by rebels, they were all granted citizenship in District 13.[14]

Culture[]

Ethnicity[]
People who lived in the Seam tended to have dark straight hair, olive skin, and gray eyes.[1] These traits were distinctive enough to residents that Katniss called it "that Seam look",[2] and she could tell them apart from merchants on sight.[8] Mrs. Everdeen and Prim looked "out of place [in the Seam]... with their light hair and blue eyes", which Katniss attributed to her mother being born into the merchant class.[1]
In a 2011 interview with Entertainment Weekly, Suzanne Collins said that Seam residents like Katniss and Gale "were not particularly intended to be biracial", nor did she have a specific ethnicity in mind for those characters while writing. However, she stated that, since The Hunger Games trilogy is set hundreds of years into the future, "There’s been a lot of ethnic mixing."[15] This suggests that those in the Seam could have mixed ethnic and/or racial backgrounds, but the details of said backgrounds are undetermined.

Lifestyle[]
 	 	 	 		 			 		 		 		 			District 12 coal miners. 		 	 
Most citizens here were coal miners; early in the morning, the streets were crowded with them as they headed to work.[1] People generally traveled on foot since few could afford cars, though some occasionally rode in wagons. Merchant and Seam children attended school and learned about their district's trade together, preparing them for employment in the mines[9] after their last reaping at 18 years old.
Due to rampant poverty, people often could not afford to eat, and many outright starved. However, families with children who took out tesserae were known to make "flat, dense loaves"[1] or "ugly drop biscuits"[16] from their year-long monthly allotments of grain. Most families in District 12 who relied on tesserae were from the Seam; the trade-off was that this increased their children's chances of being reaped for the Hunger Games.[1]
In District 12, looking old was an achievement since so many people died young, and plump people were envied because it was a sign that they weren't scraping by.[17] Despite being the smallest, poorest district in Panem, the people of 12 knew how to dance.[18]

Etymology[]
The name "Seam" comes from the actual coal seams where its citizens mined.[2]

Trivia[]
 	 	 	 		 			 		 		 		 			Jennifer Lawrence and Liam Hemsworth as Katniss and Gale in The Hunger Games film. 		 	 
The films' casting largely departed from the books' description of Seam traits. This has drawn accusations of whitewashing.[15]
Some have also speculated that, given that District 12 is located in Appalachia[9] and people from the Seam are described as typically having olive skin,[1] its residents may be Melungeon; however, it should be noted this is a derogatory term with racist and classist connotations.
Mrs. Everdeen is the only member of the merchant sector known to have moved to the Seam.[1]
Three of District 12's four victors were from the Seam: Lucy Gray Baird, Haymitch Abernathy, and Katniss Everdeen.
Of District 12's known tributes, all but two were from the Seam. The exceptions were Maysilee Donner[21] and Peeta Mellark.[10]
