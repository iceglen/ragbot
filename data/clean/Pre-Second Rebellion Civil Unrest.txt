Pre-Second Rebellion civil unrest is an unofficial term referring to the period of civil disorder around the 74th Hunger Games, preceding the Second Rebellion.

History[]
74th Hunger Games[]
Uprising of District 11[]
Main article: Uprising of District 11
In The Hunger Games film, the first uprising since the Dark Days took place in District 11 during the Hunger Games. A riot broke out in response to the death of Rue, their 12-year-old female tribute. The conflict lasted until after dark, when Peacekeeper reinforcements arrived with batons, shields, and water cannons to quell the uprising. It was presumably shut down swiftly.[1]

Conclusion of the 74th Hunger Games[]
In The Hunger Games novel, open rebellion only began to gain momentum when Katniss Everdeen and Peeta Mellark, tributes from District 12, threatened to commit double suicide by deadly nightlock berries unless they were both allowed to live.[2] This served as a major turning point for national politics, and their act of defiance unintentionally inspired rebellious sentiment across Panem.[3]


Victory Tour (74th HG)[]
When Katniss and Peeta visited District 11 on their Victory Tour, an elderly man whistled Rue's four-note song, signaling the rest of the crowd to perform District 12's three-finger salute. As the victors left the stage, this man was shot dead,[4] with several more gunshots following after once the two were inside the Justice Building.[5]
During their visits to other districts, Katniss mentioned that the crowds in Districts 3, 4, and 8 expressed "genuine elation... at the sight of [them], and under the elation, fury." She also characterized their cheers as cries for vengeance.[5]

Uprising of District 8[]
Main article: Uprising of District 8
 	 	 	 		 			 		 		 		 			A silhouetted person raising up the symbol of the mockingjay. 		 	 
In the novels, the uprising of District 8 was the first full-scale rebellion since the Dark Days.[6] It occurred during Katniss and Peeta's engagement in the Capitol on their Victory Tour. Rebels carried out a coordinated assault on centers of power and resources, temporarily overwhelming Peacekeepers until reinforcements arrived. The increased Peacekeeper response, combined with suppressive bombings, quelled the uprising within 48 hours. A week-long period of martial law followed, and when it was lifted, citizens returned to work. Many were killed when the Capitol then bombed the factory where the uprising was planned.[7]


Other acts of resistance[]
On the day before the reading of the Quarter Quell card, District 4 had been in revolt for several weeks, resulting in seafood shortages that were explained away as a consequence of bad weather. There were shortages of electric gadgets from District 3 that implied rebel action there as well, alongside rumors of uprisings in Districts 7 and 11.[8]

75th Hunger Games[]
 	 	 	 		 			 		 		 		 			The victors holding hands. 		 	 
As retaliation for the districts' unrest, President Snow announced that for the third Quarter Quell, the tributes would be reaped from "the strongest among them": the existing pool of victors.[8] This rule outraged even residents of the Capitol, which the victors capitalized on during their interviews with Caesar Flickerman, in an ultimately futile effort to put a stop to the Quell.[9] At the conclusion of the program, the victors all held hands in a rebellious display of district unity.[10]
Unbeknownst to the Capitol, rebels (including Plutarch Heavensbee) had begun to devise a plan soon after the reading of the Quell card. Tributes from Districts 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, and 11 worked in tandem to protect Katniss Everdeen and break out of the 75th Hunger Games arena. At midnight on the third night of the Games, Katniss fired an electrified arrow into the force field,[11] unknowingly allowing for her extraction to District 13 along with Finnick Odair and Beetee Latier.[12] Fifteen minutes later, Capitol bombers razed District 12, and the Second Rebellion officially began.[13]
